## Support across the testimony lifecycle

An expert opinion is not finished when the report is signed. It has to survive
a deposition, translate cleanly at mediation, and land with a jury that has
never read a wage table. Purinton Analytics structures every testifying
engagement around that full arc: the analysis is built to be examined, the
report is written to be read aloud by opposing counsel, and the testimony is
prepared as part of the engagement rather than bolted on at the end.

The firm provides vocational and life care planning testimony nationwide for
plaintiff and defense counsel, with more than 3,000 disability-related
vocational evaluations behind the methodology. Economic damages testimony is
provided by independent economist partners coordinated by the firm, each of
whom signs and defends their own analysis.

## Written reports built for scrutiny

The report is the testimony's foundation, and it is drafted on the assumption
that the closest reader will be the opposing expert. Every report states the
referral questions, the records reviewed, the instruments administered, the
data sources with editions and dates, the assumptions adopted and where they
came from, and the opinions with their reasoning. Counsel identifies the
disclosure requirements that govern the case, and the report is assembled to
support them, including the supporting materials counsel needs for service.
Nothing in the analysis depends on a source that cannot be produced.

## Deposition and trial testimony

Testimony is available in every state, in person or by remote appearance
where the court and parties permit. Remote deposition is now routine and the
firm treats it as a first-class format: tested connections, exhibits prepared
for screen sharing, and the same preparation standard as an in-person
appearance. For trial, the expert appears as the venue requires, and travel
from the firm's Kansas City base is planned at engagement so that trial dates
are protected on the calendar. Scheduling conflicts are flagged at the
conflict check rather than discovered at the pretrial conference.

Preparation is treated as part of the engagement. Before deposition, the
expert reviews the complete file, the opinions of other experts in the case,
and the intervening record, so that testimony reflects the case as it stands
rather than as it stood at the report date. Where the record has materially
changed, counsel is told before the deposition, not during it.

Trial testimony carries an additional obligation: the methodology has to be
explainable to people who have never heard of a transferable skills analysis
or a life care plan category. The firm prepares direct testimony around the
logic of the analysis rather than its vocabulary, walks the trier of fact
from records to restrictions to findings in plain sequence, and keeps
demonstrative summaries consistent with the disclosed report so that
simplification never becomes a new opinion. Jurors do not need to admire the
method; they need to be able to follow it.

## Consulting and testifying roles

Some engagements never involve a disclosed opinion. In a consulting role, the
firm evaluates the damages picture early, identifies what the records do and
do not support, assists counsel in framing examination of opposing experts,
and helps assess settlement value against the data. Discovery rules generally
treat consulting experts differently from testifying experts, and procedural
rules such as Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26 address the distinction in
federal cases, with state analogues varying by venue. Which role serves the
case, and when a consulting role should convert to a testifying one, are
legal judgments that belong entirely to counsel; the firm's practice is to
establish the designation at intake, keep the file consistent with it, and
say nothing about discoverability beyond describing how the engagement is
structured. That description is not legal advice.

## Mediation, arbitration, and settlement support

Most damages cases resolve without a verdict, and expert work earns its keep
at the table as much as on the stand. The firm supports mediation and
arbitration with concise damages summaries, plain-language explanations of
methodology for non-expert audiences, and same-day availability for questions
that arise mid-session. For settlement evaluation, the analysis distinguishes
what the record firmly supports from what remains contested, which is the
distinction a negotiation actually turns on. Where a case is heading to
arbitration, the same testimony standards apply: a written record built for
scrutiny, a witness prepared for examination, and opinions that read the
same whether the audience is an arbitrator, a mediator, or a jury.

## The typical engagement process

Testimony engagements follow the firm's four-step sequence. Counsel submits a
conflict check with the caption or parties; conflicts are cleared and
calendar availability for known deposition and trial dates is confirmed at
the same time. Scope and role, testifying or consulting, are set at intake
and the records request issues. The analysis proceeds and, in testifying
engagements, the report is delivered against the disclosure calendar.
The expert then remains engaged through deposition, mediation, pretrial
preparation, and trial, with the file maintained so that late-breaking
records are incorporated on the record rather than around it.

## Role boundaries on the stand

The witness stand is where role boundaries matter most, because
cross-examination goes straight to the edges of qualification. The firm's
principal testifies to vocational and life care planning opinions:
employability, placeability, earning capacity, labor market access, and
future care needs with their costs. He does not testify to medical causation,
life expectancy, present value, or the law, and the reports say so expressly.
Economic opinions are testified to by the independent economist partner who
signed them. An expert who declines the questions outside their discipline is
not weakened by the boundary; the discipline is what makes the answers inside
it credible.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

The firm accepts testifying engagements from plaintiffs and defendants on the
same methodology. That practice is an asset at trial: opinions
that do not bend to the retaining party are harder to impeach, and a jury
hears the difference between an expert explaining a method and an expert
defending a side. Counsel on either side of the caption receives the same
work product, the same preparation standard, and the same candor about what
the record will and will not support.
