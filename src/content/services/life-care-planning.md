## What a life care plan provides

A life care plan is a lifetime care and cost roadmap for a person living with a
catastrophic injury or a complex chronic condition. It identifies, category by
category, the physician services, therapies, medications, diagnostics, durable
medical equipment, supplies, attendant care, home modifications, and
transportation the person is expected to need; states the frequency and
duration of each item; and attaches a verifiable current cost to every line.
Done correctly, it is the factual backbone of the future care component of
damages, written so that every entry can be traced to a medical source and a
cost source.

discipline.

## The medical foundation requirement

The most common failure mode in litigation life care planning is a plan that
recommends care no physician has recommended. A life care plan is not an
independent source of medical opinion. Each recommendation in a Purinton
Analytics plan is tied to an identified foundation: treating physician records
and testimony, examining physician reports, published clinical practice
guidance for the diagnosis, or a written concurrence obtained from a qualified
provider during plan development. Where an anticipated need lacks foundation,
the plan says so rather than assuming it, because a plan that outruns its
medical support invites exclusion and drags credible entries down with weak
ones.

That discipline serves both sides of the caption. For plaintiff counsel it
produces a plan built to hold together under cross-examination. For defense
counsel it supplies the review standard: which entries in the served plan carry
documented physician support, which were generated by the planner alone, and
where frequencies or durations exceed what any medical source actually said.

## Methodology

### Records review and clinical assessment

The plan begins with the complete medical file: records from every treating
specialty, operative and imaging reports, therapy notes, medication history,
and any discharge planning or case management documentation. A structured
assessment of the evaluee follows, covering current function, activities of
daily living, supervision and safety needs, the home environment, caregiver
arrangements, and equipment and services already in use. Care already being
delivered, and already invoiced, is some of the strongest evidence of what
future care will look like, and the assessment captures it in detail.

### Collaboration with treating and examining physicians

Future care recommendations are developed in collaboration with the physicians
who know the case. Depending on access and litigation posture, that
collaboration takes the form of written questionnaires, structured interviews,
or reliance on deposition testimony and records. The plan documents which
provider supports each recommendation and in what form. When a needed
specialty has not weighed in, the plan identifies the gap for counsel instead
of papering over it, so that foundation can be obtained before disclosure
rather than challenged after it.

### Future care categories

Needs are organized into standard life care planning categories so that
nothing is double-counted and nothing is silently omitted: physician and
specialist follow-up; therapeutic services including physical, occupational,
speech, and behavioral health care; medications; laboratory and diagnostic
studies; durable medical equipment and its replacement schedule; supplies;
attendant and skilled home care; facility-based care where indicated; home
architectural modifications; transportation; and vocational or educational
services where they intersect with care. Each category states items,
frequencies, durations, start and stop ages, and replacement cycles.

### Costing and cost sources

Every item is priced with verifiable current data for the geography where care
is expected to be delivered: provider and vendor quotes, invoices for care and
equipment already in use, and published cost references. The plan cites the
source for each unit cost so that the opposing expert, the economist, and the
trier of fact can check the arithmetic and the provenance of every figure. The
plan states current costs and leaves growth rates and discounting to the
independent economist partners, keeping the economic assumptions in the signed
economic analysis where they belong.

## The typical engagement process

Life care planning engagements follow the firm's four-step sequence. First,
counsel submits a conflict check with the caption or party names; conflicts
are cleared before any substantive discussion. Second, scope is defined and a
records request tailored to the injuries is issued, with the firm's life care
plan records checklist keeping collection efficient. Third, the assessment is
completed, physician collaboration is documented, and the plan is delivered by
the agreed date with disclosure deadlines tracked from intake. Fourth, the
planner is available for deposition, mediation support, and trial testimony,
and the completed plan moves to the independent economist partner for present
value analysis when counsel requests coordinated economic work.

## Role boundaries in a life care planning engagement

A life care plan is more defensible when it is explicit about what it is not.
The planner does not diagnose, does not assign prognosis, and does not opine
on causation; those opinions belong to qualified physicians, and the plan
adopts them as foundation. The planner does not predict life expectancy;
where a physician has offered a life expectancy opinion, the plan discloses
it and the economist applies it, and where none exists, standard statistical
sources are identified for the economist's use. The planner does not reduce
future costs to present value; that analysis is performed and signed by
independent economist partners. And the planner does not offer legal
conclusions about recoverability. Each boundary is stated in the report so
the trier of fact knows exactly whose opinion supports each element.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

The same methodology serves both retention postures. Plaintiff engagements
typically build the plan: assessment, physician collaboration, categorized
needs, and cited costs, delivered in time for economic valuation and
disclosure. Defense engagements typically test a served plan: whether each
entry has documented medical foundation, whether frequencies and durations
match what providers actually said, whether items are duplicated across
categories, whether unit costs reflect the market where care will occur, and
whether the plan quietly embeds economic or medical opinions its author is not
qualified to give. Both engagements produce the same thing: a future care
record the trier of fact can rely on, whichever side offered it.
