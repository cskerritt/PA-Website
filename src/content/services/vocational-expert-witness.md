## What a vocational expert witness does

When a case puts someone's ability to work at issue, the claim has to be tested
against evidence rather than assertion. A vocational expert witness provides that
testing. The role sits between the medical record and the economic calculation.
Treating physicians and examining specialists establish diagnoses and functional
restrictions. The vocational expert determines what work, if any, remains
realistically available within those restrictions, what that work pays, and how
the injury or event has changed the person's access to the labor market. An
economist then converts any difference in earning capacity into a present value
figure.

## Methodology

A vocational opinion is only as strong as the method behind it, and the method
should look the same no matter which side issues the retention letter. Every
Purinton Analytics evaluation follows a documented sequence so that each
conclusion can be traced back through the analysis to identified records and
published data.

### Records review and clinical interview

The evaluation begins with the complete file: medical records establishing
diagnoses, restrictions, and functional limitations; education and training
records; employment records and job descriptions; wage documentation; and any
prior functional capacity evaluations, independent medical examinations, or
Social Security disability determinations. A structured clinical interview
follows, covering the work history as actually performed, education and
certifications, skills, activities of daily living, and the evaluee's own
account of current function. Where the interview and the record disagree, the
disagreement is documented and addressed in the report rather than resolved
silently.

### Vocational testing

Standardized instruments are administered when testing adds foundation to the
opinions: measures of academic achievement, aptitude, and occupational interest
selected for the referral questions at hand. Instruments and norms are
identified in the report so that opposing experts can review the scoring, and
administration follows publisher protocols, whether conducted in person or,
where the publisher supports it, remotely.

### Transferable skills analysis

Each past occupation is coded to the occupational taxonomies maintained by the
United States Department of Labor, including the Dictionary of Occupational
Titles and O\*NET. Coding captures the skill level, general educational
development, and physical demands of the work as generally performed and as
actually performed. Those demands are then compared against the documented
medical restrictions to determine which past occupations remain within capacity
and which related occupations draw on the same transferable skills at demand
levels the person can still meet. The analysis states its assumptions expressly,
including which restrictions were adopted and whose opinions they came from.

### Labor market research and wage data

Employability means little without a labor market attached to it. Once
representative occupations are identified, the analysis documents their
incidence and wages using published government data, principally the Bureau of
Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program reported at
the national, state, and metropolitan level, supplemented by state workforce
agency data and, where appropriate, direct labor market research. Pre-injury and
post-injury earning capacity are stated with sources cited so that the retained
economist, the opposing expert, and the trier of fact can trace every figure to
its origin.

### Opinion formation

Opinions address the four questions vocational testimony exists to answer.
Employability is the capacity to perform work that exists in the labor market.
Placeability is the realistic likelihood of obtaining that work given the
person's profile and the hiring market. Earning capacity is what the person
could reasonably be expected to earn before and after the event at issue. Labor
market access is the share of the market that remains open compared with the
share available before. Conclusions are expressed to a reasonable degree of
vocational certainty, findings are separated from assumptions, and the limits of
the data are stated in the report.

## The typical engagement process

Engagements follow the same four-step sequence used across the firm's practice.
First, counsel submits a conflict check with the case caption or party names;
the firm confirms availability and clears conflicts before any substantive
discussion of the case. Second, the scope of work is defined and a records
request tailored to the claims is issued, using the firm's records checklist to
keep discovery efficient. Third, the evaluation is scheduled, in person or
remote, the analysis is completed, and a written report is delivered by the
agreed date, with disclosure deadlines tracked from the outset. Fourth, the
expert is available for deposition, pretrial consultation, mediation support,
and trial testimony. Rebuttal engagements compress the same sequence to fit the
briefing schedule, and rush timelines for imminent disclosure deadlines are
accommodated when the record can responsibly support them.

## Role boundaries in a damages case

Credible damages testimony depends on each expert staying within the boundaries
of their discipline, and an evaluation is more useful to counsel when it says
plainly what it does not cover. The vocational expert does not opine on medical
causation, diagnosis, prognosis, or the permanency of restrictions; those
opinions belong to qualified physicians, and the vocational analysis adopts them
as foundation rather than creating them. The vocational expert does not
calculate the present value of future losses. When a case requires economic
damages testimony, forensic economic analyses are performed and signed by
independent economist partners coordinated by the firm, with the vocational
findings serving as one of the economist's stated inputs. Future medical and
care needs are the province of life care planning, a separate engagement with
its own methodology and its own medical foundation requirements. Finally, the
vocational expert does not offer legal conclusions. Whether a plaintiff
mitigated damages as a matter of law, or whether a claimant meets a statutory
disability standard, are questions for the court and the trier of fact; the
vocational expert supplies the factual and methodological analysis on which
those judgments rest.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

The same methodology serves both sides of the caption. For plaintiff counsel, a
vocational evaluation typically establishes the existence and extent of lost
earning capacity and grounds the economist's projections in tested, cited data.
For defense counsel, the same tools test the opposing expert's conclusions:
whether restrictions were adopted from the record or assumed, whether the
transferable skills analysis considered the full work history, whether wage
figures match published data for the relevant market, and whether the claimed
loss survives a documented mitigation analysis. The firm accepts engagements
from both sides as a deliberate practice standard, because opinions that do not
depend on the identity of the retaining party are more useful to counsel and to
the court.

## Testimony and admissibility

Vocational opinions face the reliability requirements of Federal Rule of
Evidence 702 and its state analogues, including the amendments effective
December 2023 that direct courts to ensure an expert's opinion reflects a
reliable application of principles and methods to the facts of the case. The
methodology described above is built for that scrutiny: standardized instruments
identified by name, occupational coding tied to published federal taxonomies,
wage figures traceable to government data series, and assumptions stated rather
than embedded. Reports are written to be read closely by opposing counsel and
opposing experts, and disclosure materials, including CVs and fee schedules, are
provided at retention.
