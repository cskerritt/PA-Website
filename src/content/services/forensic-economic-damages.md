## Where economic testimony fits in a damages case

By the time a jury hears a number for lost earnings or future care, three
different disciplines have contributed to it. Physicians establish diagnoses
and restrictions. Vocational and life care planning experts translate those
restrictions into earning capacity and future care needs with current costs
attached. The forensic economist takes those documented findings and answers
the final question: what is the stream of future losses worth today, in a
single present value figure, under stated and checkable assumptions?

Purinton Analytics coordinates that final step nationwide for plaintiff and
defense counsel. This page describes the methodology counsel can expect from
the analysis. The analysis itself is performed and signed by independent
economist partners coordinated by the firm.

## Performed and signed by independent economist partners

The point bears stating plainly, because it is a structural feature of the
practice and not a footnote. Jason C. Purinton is a vocational expert and
certified life care planner. He does not hold himself out as an economist,
does not perform economic damages calculations for testimony, and does not
sign economic opinions. When an engagement requires economic damages
analysis, Purinton Analytics coordinates independent economist partners,
selected for the venue and the case, who perform the analysis, sign the
report, and testify to their own work.

The separation is what makes the coordinated model defensible. Each opinion
is offered by an expert qualified in the discipline that produced it, which
is exactly what reliability standards for expert testimony require. The
economist's independence also survives cross-examination: the economist
adopts the vocational and life care planning findings as stated inputs,
identifies them as such, and remains free to apply their own professional
judgment to every economic assumption in the analysis.

## Components of the analysis

### Lost earnings and earning capacity

The foundation is the difference between what the person could have earned
without the injury or event and what they can earn with it. Pre-injury
capacity is established from actual earnings history, tax records, and the
vocational assessment of the person's trajectory; post-injury capacity comes
from the vocational evaluation's findings on remaining employability and
wages, tied to published labor market data. The economist projects both
streams over the applicable worklife and takes the difference, with every
input cited.

### Employer-provided benefits

Wages rarely travel alone. Employer contributions to health insurance,
retirement plans, and other benefits are valued from plan documents and
employment records where available, or from published benefit cost data
identified in the report, so that the loss reflects total compensation
rather than take-home pay alone.

### Worklife expectancy

How long the earnings stream would have continued is a statistical question.
The federal government discontinued its official worklife bulletins decades
ago, and contemporary practice relies on peer-reviewed tables built from
Current Population Survey data using Markov process models, notably the
tables published by Skoog, Ciecka, and Krueger in the forensic economics
literature. The signed analysis identifies the table, edition, and entry
applied, so the worklife assumption can be verified rather than taken on
faith.

### Household services

Injury and death also remove unpaid work from a household: cleaning,
maintenance, caregiving, transportation, and management of the home. The
analysis establishes lost hours from case evidence informed by published
time use data such as the American Time Use Survey, prices them at
replacement cost, and states both sources. Household services are valued
once, in the economic analysis, and the coordinated structure prevents the
same loss from appearing in two experts' reports.

### Future care costs

Where a life care plan or medical cost projection exists, the economist
carries its current-dollar cost stream forward with stated medical cost
growth assumptions and discounts it to present value. The care needs and
their pricing remain the life care planner's opinion; the growth and
discounting are the economist's. The two reports cite each other expressly
so the trier of fact can follow the chain.

### Present value and discounting

Every future stream is reduced to present value using market interest rate
data, with the discount approach and its sources stated in the report.
Jurisdictional conventions on discounting and taxation differ, and the
signed analysis identifies which convention it applies and why, as a
methodological disclosure rather than a legal position.

## The typical engagement process

Coordinated economic engagements follow the firm's four-step sequence.
Counsel submits a conflict check; the firm clears it and confirms an
independent economist partner for the venue. Scope and records requests
follow, coordinated so tax, wage, and benefit records arrive alongside the
vocational and life care planning work the economist will consume. The
economist performs and signs the analysis on a schedule tracked against
disclosure deadlines. The economist then sits for deposition and trial
testimony on their own opinion, with logistics coordinated through the firm
so counsel manages one calendar instead of three.

## Role boundaries in economic damages work

The boundaries run in both directions. The economist does not decide what
work a person can perform, what wages that work pays in the local market, or
what care the person will need; those findings arrive from the vocational
and life care planning analyses and are adopted as stated inputs. The
vocational expert and life care planner, in turn, do not project growth
rates, select discount rates, or compute present value; those judgments
belong to the economist who signs them. Neither side of the boundary offers
medical or legal conclusions. Reports that respect these lines are easier to
defend and harder to exclude, which is why the firm builds every coordinated
engagement around them.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

The coordinated economic practice serves both sides of the caption. For
plaintiff counsel, the signed analysis converts documented vocational and
care findings into a present value figure that can withstand methodological
scrutiny. For defense counsel, independent economist partners test served
economic reports: whether growth and discount assumptions are internally
consistent and sourced, whether the worklife table cited actually says what
the report claims, whether household services were double-counted, and
whether the arithmetic reconciles. The methodology is the same either way,
which is the point of retaining objective experts in the first place.
