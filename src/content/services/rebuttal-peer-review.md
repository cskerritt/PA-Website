## Testing a report against its own discipline

The strongest critique of an expert report rarely comes from an opposing
theory of the case. It comes from holding the report to the standards its
author's own profession publishes: the certification body's standards of
practice, the peer-reviewed methodology literature, and the government data
sources the discipline itself designates as authoritative. When a report
fails that comparison, the failure is not a matter of dueling opinions; it is
a demonstrable gap between what the expert did and what the expert's field
says should be done.

Purinton Analytics performs that comparison for plaintiff and defense counsel
nationwide. Vocational evaluations and life care plans are reviewed directly
by the firm's principal within his certified disciplines. Economic damages
reports are reviewed by independent economist partners coordinated by the
firm, who perform and sign their own critiques. The output, in every
configuration, is a review counsel can rely on because it would read the same
no matter which side requested it.

## The four-part critique framework

### Foundation

Every opinion in a damages report rests on something, and the first question
is what. The review traces each conclusion to its claimed support: were the
functional restrictions adopted from physician records or assumed by the
expert? Does each life care plan entry carry an identified medical source, or
did the planner prescribe care no provider recommended? Did the economist's
earning capacity figures come from a vocational analysis, or from an
unexamined assumption about the person's trajectory? Opinions that
manufacture their own foundation are the most vulnerable finding a review can
produce, because the gap is visible to a jury without any technical
explanation.

### Data vintage and sources

The second question is whether the data behind the report is current, fit for
the purpose, and used as its publisher intends. Wage figures are checked
against the cited government series and its most recent release. Occupational
findings are checked against the current federal taxonomies rather than
superseded editions. Worklife, life expectancy, and cost tables are checked
for edition and applicability to the person being evaluated. A report built
on data that was replaced years before the report was signed has a vintage
problem its author will have to defend, and the review documents exactly
which sources aged out and what the current sources show.

### Method and math

The third question is whether the report's own numbers reconcile. The review
recomputes what can be recomputed: arithmetic in the cost tables, consistency
between narrative statements and exhibit figures, whether stated growth and
discount assumptions were actually applied as stated, and whether totals
carry through from schedule to summary. It also examines whether the claimed
methodology matches published practice for the discipline. Calculation
defects are individually small and collectively corrosive; a report that
cannot add is hard to rehabilitate.

### Role boundaries

The final question is whether the expert stayed inside their qualification. A
vocational expert projecting present values, a life care planner assigning
life expectancy, an economist rendering employability findings without a
vocational foundation, a planner embedding medical opinions no physician
gave: each is an opinion delivered by someone unqualified to give it. The
review flags every boundary crossing, because those opinions are candidates
for exclusion under reliability standards and because they invite the jury to
question the discipline of the entire report.

## The tone of a usable critique

A review is only as persuasive as it is disciplined. The deliverable is
written as a methodological analysis, not an attack: each finding states what
the reviewed report did, what the discipline's published standard calls for,
and what the difference means for the reliability of the opinion, with the
sources cited so the reader can verify the comparison. Findings that would
not survive the same scrutiny applied to the reviewer's own work are not
made. That restraint is tactical as much as professional, because a critique
that overreaches gives the opposing expert an easy rehabilitation and gives
the jury a reason to discount the sound findings along with the strained
ones.

## Rebuttal or consulting review

The same analysis can be delivered in two postures. As a disclosed rebuttal,
the reviewing expert signs a report and testifies to the critique. As a
consulting review, the analysis informs counsel's examination and case
evaluation without a disclosed opinion. Discovery treats the two postures
differently, and choosing between them is counsel's legal judgment to make,
not the expert's; the firm's role is to state the findings accurately in
whichever form the engagement requires and to keep the file consistent with
the posture chosen from the outset.

## Rush engagements

Rebuttal work runs on the disclosure calendar, which is usually short by the
time the opposing report arrives. Rush review is a standing part of the
firm's practice: feasibility is assessed candidly at the conflict check, the
scope is trimmed to the opinions that matter, and delivery is scheduled
against the deadline rather than the other way around. For imminent
deadlines, counsel should call the office directly rather than wait on
written intake.

## The typical engagement process

Review engagements follow the firm's four-step sequence at rebuttal speed.
Counsel submits a conflict check identifying the parties and the expert under
review. Scope is set: which opinions, which posture, which deadline, and the
report, file materials, and underlying records are collected. The critique is
completed and delivered as a signed rebuttal report or a consulting
memorandum. Where disclosed, the reviewing expert then sits for deposition
and trial testimony on the critique, including, where engaged through the
coordinated structure, the independent economist partner who signed the
economic review.

## Both sides of the caption

Review work is where neutrality proves itself. The four questions above are
asked identically of plaintiff and defense reports, and the firm accepts
review engagements from both sides as a matter of standing practice. That
history is itself useful to counsel: a reviewing expert who critiques the
same defects regardless of who retained them is far harder to paint as an
advocate, and the critique carries the weight of a standard rather than a
side.
