## What a medical cost projection is

Not every case with future medical damages needs a comprehensive life care
plan, and serving one where a narrower analysis would do inflates cost and
invites a proportionality fight. A medical cost projection answers a smaller
question with the same rigor: given the treatment that qualified providers
have actually recommended, what will that care cost, at verifiable prices, in
the market where it will be delivered, over the period it is expected to
continue?

The projection is built from the medical record rather than around it. It
inventories the recommended care, confirms the provider source for each item,
assigns frequencies and durations from the recommendations themselves, and
prices each service with cited, checkable data. The output is a cost stream
counsel can use directly in negotiation or hand to an independent economist
partner for present value analysis when the case requires a signed economic
opinion.

The document earns its keep at specific decision points: pricing a demand or
an offer before either side has committed to full expert workups, giving a
mediator a documented number to work with, and testing whether the future
medical component of a claim is proportionate to the record behind it. In
each of those settings the projection's value is the same: every figure in it
can be checked by the other side, which is what separates a negotiating
position from a negotiating exhibit.

## Projection or life care plan: choosing the right instrument

The choice between a projection and a full life care plan is a scope decision,
and getting it wrong in either direction damages the case. A projection that
tries to cover a catastrophic injury will miss care categories, understate
duration, and collapse under cross-examination when the gaps surface. A full
plan built for a moderate soft-tissue case overstates the analysis the record
supports and hands opposing counsel an overreach argument.

The dividing lines are practical. A projection fits when the future care is
defined, documented, and bounded: a recommended surgery with rehabilitation,
a medication regimen with periodic specialist follow-up, a course of
injections with imaging. A full life care plan is required when needs are
lifelong and span multiple categories, when the evaluee is a child or has a
progressive condition so that needs will change over time, when attendant
care or home modification is in play, or when equipment carries replacement
cycles that a fixed-period estimate cannot represent. Purinton Analytics
states this assessment in writing at intake, and when the record shows a
projection cannot responsibly carry the case, the engagement is scoped as a
life care plan instead, before the work is built on the wrong foundation.

## Methodology

### Defining the anticipated care

The projection begins by extracting every future care recommendation from the
record: operative candidacy notes, referral orders, therapy prescriptions,
medication plans, and physician deposition testimony. Each item enters the
projection with its source identified. Care that appears in claims or demand
materials but not in any provider record is flagged rather than priced, and
that flag is itself a finding counsel can use.

### Geographic cost research

Health care pricing varies widely by market, so a projection priced at
national averages misstates the loss in either direction. Each service is
priced for the geography where the person is expected to receive treatment,
using verifiable sources: claims-based benchmark data of the kind published
by independent organizations such as FAIR Health, fee schedule references
maintained by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services used as stated
reference points, and facility and provider pricing in the relevant market.
The report identifies each source and its vintage so that the data can be
checked, and stale pricing is treated as a defect, not a shortcut.

### Stating the limits

Every projection carries an express scope statement: the period covered, the
recommendations included, the items excluded and why, and the assumptions
adopted from the medical record. Inflation of future medical prices and
discounting to present value are economic opinions, so the projection
presents current-dollar costs and identifies the stream for the independent
economist partners to carry forward in a separately signed analysis. A
projection that quietly embeds growth and discount assumptions has crossed
into another discipline, and that line is kept bright.

## The typical engagement process

Projection engagements follow the firm's standard four-step sequence,
compressed to match the narrower scope. Counsel submits a conflict check and
the firm clears it before substantive discussion. Scope is defined and the
defining records are requested, usually a focused set: the operative
recommendation, current therapy orders, medication list, and recent billing.
The analysis is completed and the written projection delivered by the agreed
date. The projecting expert then remains available for deposition and trial
testimony, and for coordination with the vocational and economic components
when the case grows into a coordinated damages engagement.

## Role boundaries in a projection engagement

A medical cost projection prices care; it does not prescribe it. The
recommendations belong to treating and examining providers, and the
projection adopts them with citations rather than generating its own. Life
expectancy and survival questions belong to physicians and to the statistical
sources the economist applies. Present value, growth rates, and discount
rates are performed and signed by independent economist partners coordinated
by the firm. Vocational impact, including whether the same injuries limit
work and earnings, is a separate vocational evaluation with its own
methodology. The projection states each of these boundaries so the trier of
fact can see whose opinion supports every number.

## Plaintiff and defense uses

Plaintiff counsel typically engage a projection to ground settlement demands
and mediation positions in documented, priced care rather than round numbers.
Defense counsel engage the same analysis to test claimed future medicals:
whether each item traces to an actual provider recommendation, whether the
claimed prices match the venue's market data, and whether the served analysis
respected the line between medical cost research and economic opinion. The
methodology does not change with the retaining party, which is precisely what
makes the resulting numbers useful in a negotiation where both sides can
check the sources.
