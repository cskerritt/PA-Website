## The earning capacity question in family court

Support disputes frequently stall on a single factual disagreement: one party
says they cannot earn more, and the other says they will not. Statutes and
guidelines in most states let courts resolve that disagreement by looking to
earning capacity rather than reported income, but capacity has to be proven,
not asserted. A judge asked to impute income needs evidence of what work the
party is qualified to perform, whether that work exists in the relevant
market, what it pays, and how quickly it could realistically be obtained. A
judge asked not to impute needs the same evidence read honestly in the other
direction. That is vocational evidence, and producing it with documented
methodology is what Purinton Analytics does in family law matters nationwide.

The firm serves either party's counsel and accepts neutral appointments. The
methodology is identical in every posture, which is precisely what makes the
resulting testimony useful in a courtroom where the other side gets to
cross-examine.

## How the evaluation works

A family law earning capacity evaluation follows the same documented sequence
the firm applies in injury litigation, adapted to the support context. It
begins with records: education, training, licensure, complete work history
with duties and wages, recent earnings documentation, and any clinical records
offered to support claimed restrictions. A structured interview covers the
work actually performed, skills used, the circumstances of workforce exit
where applicable, and current activities. Standardized testing is added when
it strengthens foundation, particularly for parties long out of the workforce
whose academic skill levels are genuinely uncertain.

A transferable skills analysis then codes the work history to the
occupational taxonomies maintained by the United States Department of Labor
and identifies the occupations the person's skills and education support
today, at what level of demand. The labor market half of the analysis
documents what those occupations pay in the relevant venue, using the Bureau
of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program,
Current Population Survey data on participation patterns, state workforce
data, and direct market research where the locality requires it. The opinion
states earning capacity as a documented figure or range with every step
traceable, identifies the timeline over which capacity is realistically
reachable, and flags any claimed restriction that lacks clinical support,
whichever party that finding favors.

## The reentering spouse

The most common family law referral involves a spouse who left the workforce
for caregiving and is now, in the court's eyes, expected to return to it. An
honest evaluation neither freezes this person at their last paycheck from a
decade ago nor waves them into their former career at former wages. Skills
date. Licenses lapse. Industries move. The evaluation credits what remains
current, identifies what reentry actually looks like at today's hiring
standards, and where retraining is the realistic bridge, documents specific
programs, durations, and costs rather than gesturing at education in the
abstract. Courts consistently find stepwise capacity opinions of this kind
more useful than flat numbers, because they map onto the phased support
orders many jurisdictions favor.

## Testing claimed limitations

When a party claims they cannot work or cannot earn beyond a level, the
evaluation tests the claim against the record. Clinical restrictions are
adopted from qualified providers, never created by the evaluator, and the
analysis is explicit about which restrictions have documentation and which
have only assertion. Demonstrated capacity is compared against current
earnings: a professional credential in active standing, a recent work history
at higher levels, or ongoing informal work all bear on whether the gap
between capacity and reported income reflects the labor market or a choice.
The evaluation reports the gap and its evidentiary basis. Whether that gap
amounts to voluntary underemployment as a legal matter is the court's
question, and the report is careful to leave it there.

## Boundaries, posture, and testimony

The firm's role in family law is deliberately narrow. No opinion is offered
on what support should be ordered, how property should be divided, or how a
guideline should be applied; those are legal questions. No medical or
psychological diagnoses are made. Economic projections beyond the vocational
opinion, where a matter needs them, are performed and signed by independent
economist partners coordinated by the firm. What remains is the thing courts
actually lack in these disputes: a documented, data-anchored answer to what
this person can earn. Evaluations are available nationwide, in person or
remotely as testing protocols permit, with testimony at deposition, hearing,
and trial in either appearance format the court allows.

Family court calendars also shape the work product. Support hearings move
faster than tort litigation, temporary orders create interim deadlines, and
judges reading a vocational report between a full docket's other matters
need conclusions they can locate in a page, not an appendix. Reports are
structured accordingly: a stated opinion with its capacity figure or range
up front, the methodology and data behind it laid out for examination, and
exhibits organized so that counsel can walk the court through the basis in
minutes. Engagements begin with the firm's documented conflict check, which
in domestic matters includes both parties and, where applicable, closely
held businesses either spouse controls.
