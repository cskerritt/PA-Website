## The shape of a wrongful death damages case

A wrongful death action asks a court to value what a person's death took from
the people who depended on them. The non-economic dimensions of that loss are
for the trier of fact. The economic dimensions are for experts, and they are
more demanding than they first appear. The decedent's paycheck at death is a
data point, not an answer. The real questions are what the decedent's earning
capacity was, how it would probably have developed, how long the decedent
would have worked, what benefits attached to that work, what the decedent
consumed personally, and what services the decedent provided at home that now
must be replaced. Every one of those questions has a methodology, and every
methodology has published data behind it.

Purinton Analytics supplies the vocational and evidentiary foundation of that
analysis nationwide and coordinates the valuation itself, which is performed
and signed by independent economist partners. The firm works for plaintiff
counsel building survivor claims and for defense counsel testing them, with
the same documented approach either way.

## Establishing the earnings foundation

The foundation of any credible loss analysis is the decedent's demonstrated
earning history and the capacity it evidences. The vocational review
assembles tax returns, wage statements, and personnel records into an
earnings base that reflects how the decedent actually worked: steady
employment, seasonal patterns, overtime dependence, self-employment cycles,
or a career still in its early trajectory. Education, training, licensure,
and promotion history establish what the decedent was qualified to do and
what advancement the record supports. Where a claimed trajectory outruns the
documentation, the analysis says so. Where the documentation supports more
than the last paycheck shows, such as a completed credential not yet
reflected in wages, the analysis says that too, with sources.

Published data disciplines the process at every step. Occupational earnings
are checked against the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment
and Wage Statistics program for the decedent's occupation and region.
Benefits claims are tied to plan documents rather than rules of thumb. The
result is an earnings foundation the economist partner can adopt as a stated
input and that either side can trace line by line.

## Worklife, life expectancy, and the tables

How long the decedent would have worked and lived are statistical questions
answered from published tables: life tables from the National Center for
Health Statistics and the Social Security Administration, and worklife
expectancy figures derived from published labor force participation data.
Selecting among them, adjusting for the decedent's characteristics, and
applying them in the loss calculation is economic work, performed and signed
by the independent economist partner. The vocational assessment contributes
the documented characteristics that drive those selections: age, education,
occupation, attachment to the labor force, and any documented health factors
already in the record. Keeping that division clean means each expert
testifies only to what their discipline actually supports.

## Household services and the second job

Most decedents held two jobs, only one of which produced a W-2. Child care,
meal preparation, home and vehicle maintenance, yard work, bookkeeping, and
the management of a household are services with measurable replacement value,
and in many families they are a large share of the true economic loss. The
firm documents the services profile through structured interviews with
survivors and cross-references it against national time use benchmarks
published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, so the claimed hours are anchored
to data as well as memory. The economist partner then values the documented
services. On defense engagements the same tools test a served services claim
for inflation beyond what the household composition and record support.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

For plaintiff counsel, the firm builds the affirmative record: an earnings
foundation with no unexplained gaps, a services profile that survives
cross-examination, and a coordinated economic analysis signed by an
independent economist. For defense counsel, the firm audits what has been
served: whether the earnings base cherry-picks the best years, whether growth
and trajectory assumptions have record support, whether worklife and
consumption were handled consistently, and whether the services claim matches
the household's documented reality. The firm accepts both engagements as a
matter of practice policy, and the neutrality is not decorative. An analysis
that would read the same no matter who paid for it is the only kind worth
serving in a death case, where the numbers will be examined in the hardest
possible light.

## Process and timing

Wrongful death engagements begin with a documented conflict check, followed by
scope definition and a records request built around the decedent's work and
household life. Analysis and reporting are scheduled against the disclosure
calendar, and the firm coordinates timing with the independent economist
partner so the vocational foundation and the valuation arrive as one coherent,
consistently documented package. Deposition and trial testimony are available
nationwide, in person or by remote appearance where the court permits.
