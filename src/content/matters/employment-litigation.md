## Where vocational evidence decides employment cases

Liability gets the attention in employment litigation, but damages are where
many of these cases are actually decided, and employment damages are built on
vocational questions. Back pay runs from separation until reemployment cures
the loss or should have. Front pay depends on how long a compensation gap
will persist. Both turn on facts a vocational expert is equipped to
establish: what employment is genuinely comparable to the lost position, what
the market offered during the loss period, whether the plaintiff's search was
reasonably diligent, and when a diligent search should have produced
comparable work. Purinton Analytics provides that analysis nationwide, for
plaintiffs documenting a real and persistent loss and for defendants testing
a claimed one, with the resulting loss calculations performed and signed by
independent economist partners.

## Defining comparable employment

Mitigation doctrine generally requires reasonable efforts toward
substantially equivalent employment, which makes the definition of
comparability the load-bearing wall of the analysis. Title alone is not a
definition. The analysis codes the lost position to the occupational
taxonomies maintained by the United States Department of Labor, capturing
duties, skill level, supervisory scope, and compensation structure as the job
was actually performed. From that coding, the analysis identifies the
occupations that draw on the same transferable skills at comparable levels
and documents their incidence and wages in the relevant labor market using
the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics
program and state workforce data. The product is a concrete, cited picture of
the employment the plaintiff could reasonably have pursued, which disciplines
both an inflated front pay claim and an unrealistic mitigation defense.

## Testing the search

The search record is examined against that market picture. On the effort
side, the analysis reviews applications, correspondence, agency and
networking contacts, resume quality and targeting, and testimony about
method and intensity. On the market side, it documents openings and hiring
conditions during the actual loss period, using published series such as the
Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey alongside occupation-specific data.
The two sides are read together. A modest number of applications may be
entirely reasonable in a thin specialized market; a large number of scattershot
applications may still be an unreasonable search if it ignored the comparable
market that existed. The opinion addresses diligence in context, states the
evidence for each finding, and avoids treating hindsight as method.

## Reemployment timelines and persisting gaps

When did the loss end, or when should it have? The analysis develops an
evidence-based reemployment timeline from the worker's occupation, level,
age, geography, and the documented market conditions of the loss period,
informed by published data on unemployment duration. Where the plaintiff has
been reemployed, the analysis addresses whether the new position cures the
loss: comparable duties at lower pay, a step down in level, lost benefits, or
a longer path back to the prior trajectory are findings that shape front pay.
Where reemployment has not occurred, the analysis states the realistic
horizon and its basis. These findings become stated inputs to the back pay
and front pay calculations performed and signed by the independent economist
partner, keeping the employability opinion and the valuation opinion in
separately credentialed hands.

## Remote work and the shape of the comparable market

The growth of remote and hybrid work has changed what the comparable market
means for many occupations, and a mitigation analysis that ignores it is
analyzing a market that no longer exists. For occupations with substantial
remote incidence, the pool of comparable openings extends beyond commuting
distance, which can support a defense argument that more was available and,
with equal force, a plaintiff argument that a national remote search was
conducted and still came up short. The analysis addresses remote scope
occupation by occupation, using published data and posting evidence rather
than assumption, and states how the market was defined so the definition
itself can be examined.

## Plaintiff and defense engagements

The firm accepts employment engagements from both sides as standing practice.
For plaintiff counsel, the work documents that the search was real, the
market was tested, and the remaining gap is attributable to the separation
rather than to inaction. For defense counsel, the work tests the claim:
whether the search met the market that actually existed, whether claimed
comparability is too narrow or too broad, and whether the loss period claimed
outruns the documented timeline evidence. Statutory contexts differ, from
claims before the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission to state law
actions, and the analysis stays descriptive about frameworks while leaving
legal standards to counsel and the court.

## Process, boundaries, and testimony

Engagements begin with a documented conflict check and a scope conversation,
because employment cases vary widely in what they need; some require a full
employability evaluation with testing, others a focused mitigation review of
the file. The vocational expert does not opine on whether discrimination or
retaliation occurred, does not calculate damages figures, and does not offer
legal conclusions about the duty to mitigate; the analysis supplies the
factual and labor market foundation on which those arguments and calculations
rest. Reports are delivered against the disclosure calendar, and deposition
and trial testimony are available nationwide, in person or by remote
appearance.
