## Damages at scale without shortcuts

Product liability litigation ranges from a single plaintiff injured by a
single machine to multidistrict proceedings consolidating thousands of
claims before one court. The damages problem changes shape across that
range, but it never goes away. Every claimant who asserts lost earning
capacity puts a vocational question in play, and in a mass proceeding those
questions arrive by the hundred. The temptation at scale is to substitute
assumption for evaluation: to model an inventory on averages and hope the
individual files are never opened. Both sides know the files eventually get
opened. The alternative is a protocol: a documented, repeatable evaluation
method that produces individually signed, individually founded opinions at a
pace an inventory schedule can absorb.

Purinton Analytics provides that protocol work nationwide, in single-product
cases and mass tort inventories, for plaintiff and defense counsel. Where a
matter needs economic valuation, at the claimant level or across an
inventory, the analysis is performed and signed by independent economist
partners coordinated by the firm.

## What a common protocol contains

A protocol is fixed in writing before the first claimant is evaluated, and it
governs everything that should not vary from claimant to claimant. Records
review follows the same checklist, built around the litigation's fact sheets
and medical templates. The structured interview follows the same guide.
Standardized instruments, where the referral questions call for testing, are
the same instruments administered under the same publisher protocols.
Occupational coding uses the same Department of Labor taxonomies, and wage
findings draw on the same Bureau of Labor Statistics series, at documented
vintages, applied to each claimant's own labor market. Report architecture is
identical, so that any two reports in the inventory can be laid side by side
and compared finding by finding.

What the protocol deliberately does not standardize is the findings. Each
claimant's restrictions come from that claimant's medical record. Each work
history, each transferable skills profile, and each earning capacity
conclusion is that claimant's own. The protocol makes differences between
claimants visible and explainable, which is exactly what distinguishes a
method from a template, and it gives the eventual cross-examiner a stated
standard to test each report against rather than a moving target.

## Why consistency is a legal asset

Expert evidence in federal product litigation answers to Rule 702 and its
reliability requirements, and inventory litigation multiplies the exposure: a
methodological flaw in one report is a flaw in every report built the same
way. A written protocol confronts that risk directly. The method is stated
once, defended once, and applied consistently, so challenges resolve into
questions about application to a particular claimant rather than
case-by-case attacks on shifting methodology. For defense counsel auditing
an opposing inventory, the same logic runs in reverse: reports that claim a
common method but drift in application, or apply uniform conclusions to
non-uniform records, are identified by comparing them against their own
stated standard, which the firm's review memoranda do claimant by claimant.

## Bellwethers and sequencing

In multidistrict proceedings, early trials are read as signals for the
inventory behind them, which raises the stakes of individual damages
evidence. The firm supports bellwether workups with full individual
evaluations performed under the inventory protocol, so that the damages
evidence presented in the trial cases rests on the same method that would
apply to the rest of the docket. Scheduling is built around the case
management orders that govern the proceeding, including tranche deadlines,
examination stipulations, and discovery windows, and the firm confirms
evaluation capacity for each tranche at the conflict check stage rather than
discovering limits mid-schedule.

## Boundaries and the coordinated team

The firm's lane in product litigation is damages. Defect, causation, warnings
adequacy, and regulatory compliance belong to engineering, scientific, and
medical experts, and the vocational work adopts the medical record rather
than supplementing it. Aggregate settlement valuation and allocation are
counsel's work; the firm's contribution is the claimant-level foundation that
makes an aggregate position defensible. Where claimants' injuries create
future care needs, life care planning is available under the same
protocol-driven approach, and all economic conversion, from a single
claimant's present value to inventory-wide loss figures, is performed and
signed by independent economist partners. Engagements begin with a
documented conflict check covering the full party structure of the
proceeding, and testimony is available nationwide, in person or by remote
appearance.

## Data vintage across a long docket

Mass proceedings run for years, and government data series are revised while
they run. A protocol that silently mixes wage data vintages across tranches
invites the charge that later reports were built on different foundations
than the ones already examined. The firm's protocols therefore state the
vintage of every occupational and wage series in use, apply one vintage
within each tranche, and document any update between tranches together with
its effect. When an opposing inventory model mixes vintages without saying
so, that finding goes in the review memorandum, because in litigation at
scale, quiet inconsistencies compound into structural ones.
