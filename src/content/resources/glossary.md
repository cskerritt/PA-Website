## Vocational evaluation terms

### Employability

The capacity to perform work that exists in the labor market, given a person's
residual functional capacity, education, training, and skills. Employability
asks whether qualifying work exists; it does not by itself ask whether the
person would realistically be hired.

### Placeability

The realistic likelihood that a person will actually obtain work within their
capacity, considering employer hiring practices, the person's presentation and
history, labor market conditions, and any accommodations required. A report
that treats employability and placeability as the same question has skipped a
step.

### Earning capacity

What a person could reasonably be expected to earn, before and after the event
at issue, based on capacity rather than on any single job. Earning capacity is
an ability measure; actual earnings are evidence of it, not the same thing.

### Loss of earning capacity

The difference between pre-event and post-event earning capacity. Distinct
from lost wages, which measure specific earnings missed during a defined
period. A person can have modest lost wages and a large loss of earning
capacity, or the reverse.

### Labor market access

The share of the labor market open to a person compared with the share
available before the event, given restrictions, skills, and qualifications.
Often expressed as a reduction in the number or percentage of occupations the
person can perform.

### Transferable skills analysis (TSA)

A structured comparison of the skills, aptitudes, and demands of a person's
past work against the requirements of other occupations, using the
occupational taxonomies of the United States Department of Labor. The TSA
identifies which occupations remain within capacity and draw on skills the
person already has.

### Labor market survey

Direct research into the availability, requirements, and wages of identified
occupations in a defined geographic market, typically through employer
contacts and published postings, documented so the survey can be checked.

### Vocational testing

Standardized instruments measuring academic achievement, aptitude, interests,
or related constructs, administered under publisher protocols and interpreted
against identified norms. Instruments and norms should be named in the report.

### Residual functional capacity

What a person can still do despite medically documented limitations, stated in
terms of exertional and non-exertional capacities. In litigation, the
vocational expert adopts restrictions from identified medical sources rather
than creating them.

### Exertional level

The strength demand classification of work as defined in the Department of
Labor's occupational system: sedentary, light, medium, heavy, and very heavy.
Comparing a job's exertional level against documented restrictions is a basic
step in employability analysis.

### Specific Vocational Preparation (SVP)

The Department of Labor's measure of the time required to learn the techniques
and develop the facility needed for average performance in an occupation,
ranging from short demonstration to years of preparation. SVP bears on
retraining feasibility and skill transferability.

### Mitigation

Efforts by an injured or separated person to reduce their losses, typically
through job search, retraining, or accepting available work. Vocational
analysis addresses the reasonableness and results of those efforts; whether
mitigation was legally sufficient is a question for the trier of fact.

### Pediatric earning capacity analysis

Assessment of the probable earning capacity of a child without a work history,
built from the child's documented development, family and educational factors,
and published data on educational attainment and earnings. The methodology is
necessarily probabilistic and should say so.

## Medical-legal terms

### Maximum medical improvement (MMI)

The point at which a condition has stabilized and further significant
functional improvement is not expected with additional treatment. Evaluations
performed before MMI document the anticipated course and note which findings
may change.

### Functional capacity evaluation (FCE)

A structured, typically therapist-administered assessment of physical work
capacities such as lifting, carrying, standing, and sustained activity. An FCE
is one source of restrictions a vocational expert or life care planner may
rely on, with its source and date identified.

### Independent medical examination (IME)

An examination performed by a physician retained for evaluation rather than
treatment, usually at the request of one party. IME findings enter the
vocational and life care planning analyses the same way treating opinions do:
as attributed medical sources.

### Permanent restrictions

Activity limitations a physician states are expected to persist. Because the
vocational and life care planning analyses build on restrictions, whose
restrictions were adopted, and from which document, should be traceable in
every report.

### Activities of daily living (ADLs)

Basic self-care and household functions such as bathing, dressing, meal
preparation, and home management. ADL capacity bears on attendant care needs
in a life care plan and on household services losses in an economic analysis.

## Life care planning terms

### Life care plan

A comprehensive, clinically founded projection of the medical care, therapies,
attendant care, equipment, supplies, and environmental needs of a person with
a permanent injury or chronic condition, with frequencies, durations, and
costs stated item by item from disclosed sources.

### Medical cost projection

A narrower costing of defined, anticipated medical needs over a defined
period, without the full evaluation scope of a life care plan. Appropriate for
bounded questions; not a substitute for a life care plan where lifetime,
multi-domain needs are at issue.

### Clinical foundation

The documented medical basis for each recommendation in a life care plan:
treating or examining provider recommendations, or collaboration with
qualified physicians during planning. Items without clinical foundation are
the first target of a competent review.

### Attendant care

Paid or family-provided personal assistance with daily functions, stated in
hours per day and level of care. Attendant care is frequently the largest cost
category in a catastrophic life care plan and a common site of double counting
against household services claims.

### Durable medical equipment (DME)

Reusable medical equipment such as wheelchairs, seating systems, lifts, and
hospital beds. Plans state acquisition costs and replacement cycles, which in
pediatric cases must account for growth.

## Economic damages terms

Terms in this group describe analyses that are performed and signed by
independent economist partners coordinated by the firm.

### Present value

The amount of money today that, invested at assumed rates, would fund a stream
of future losses as they come due. Reducing future losses to present value is
the economist's core calculation, and the assumptions behind it should be
stated and supported.

### Discount rate

The interest rate used to convert future dollars into present value. Paired
with growth assumptions, the discount rate drives the size of a present value
figure, which is why reports disclose the rate chosen and its source.

### Worklife expectancy

The expected remaining years of labor force participation for a person of a
given age, sex, and education, drawn from published worklife tables such as
the Markov-model tables published in the forensic economics literature.
Distinct from life expectancy: people generally stop working before they stop
living.

### Life expectancy

The expected remaining years of life, drawn from published life tables such as
the United States Life Tables published by the National Center for Health
Statistics. Where a party contends an injury changed life expectancy, that
opinion belongs to a physician, not to the economist or planner who uses it.

### Fringe benefits

The non-wage components of compensation, such as employer retirement
contributions and health coverage, included in a loss calculation when the
record documents them. Also called employer-paid benefits.

### Household services

The economic value of services a person provided to their household, such as
cleaning, maintenance, and caregiving, measured from what changed after the
event and valued using published time-use and wage data such as the American
Time Use Survey.

### Back pay and front pay

In employment matters, back pay is the compensation lost between the adverse
action and judgment or resolution; front pay is the loss projected forward
after that point. Economic testimony typically supports the calculation while
the legal availability of each remedy is for the court.

## Data sources and taxonomies

### Dictionary of Occupational Titles (DOT)

The Department of Labor's legacy taxonomy of occupations, defining exertional
levels, SVP, and worker trait profiles. Though no longer updated, it remains
in wide forensic and administrative use, and reports should say which taxonomy
they used and why.

### O\*NET

The Department of Labor's current occupational information system, describing
occupations by tasks, skills, abilities, and work context. Commonly used
alongside the DOT in transferable skills analysis.

### Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS)

The Bureau of Labor Statistics program publishing employment counts and wage
distributions by occupation at the national, state, and metropolitan level.
A standard source for wage foundations in vocational and economic reports,
cited with its reference year.

### American Time Use Survey (ATUS)

The Bureau of Labor Statistics survey measuring how people spend their time,
including household production. A standard statistical foundation for valuing
household services.

## Testimony and disclosure terms

### Federal Rule of Evidence 702

The federal standard governing admissibility of expert testimony, requiring
sufficient facts or data, reliable principles and methods, and a reliable
application of those methods to the case, as assessed by the court. State
analogues vary, and the operative standard is a legal question for counsel.

### Expert disclosure

The formal identification of an expert and, for retained experts in federal
court, the written report stating opinions, bases, data considered,
qualifications, prior testimony, and compensation. Timing is set by the
scheduling order or by rule.

### Rebuttal report

An expert report offered to contradict or rebut another party's expert
disclosure, typically on a compressed deadline. Sound rebuttal work tests the
opposing report against its own cited sources and concedes what is solid.

### Total disability based on individual unemployability (TDIU)

A Department of Veterans Affairs benefit standard under which a veteran may be
compensated at the total disability rate when service-connected disabilities
prevent substantially gainful employment even though the combined schedular
rating is less than total. Vocational evidence on employability is frequently
central to TDIU questions.

### Own-occupation and any-occupation

Disability insurance coverage standards: own-occupation policies ask whether
the insured can perform their own occupation, while any-occupation policies
ask whether the insured can perform any occupation for which they are
reasonably suited. Long-term disability disputes often turn on the transition
between the two, and vocational analysis addresses both.
