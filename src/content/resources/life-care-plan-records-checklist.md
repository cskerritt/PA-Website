## How to use this checklist

A life care plan projects the medical and support needs of a person with a
permanent injury or condition across the balance of life, and every item in the
plan must trace to a clinical foundation. This checklist organizes the records
that build that foundation. Print it, mark each item as available, requested,
or not applicable, and send it with the referral. The intake call produces a
records request tailored to the injuries and claims in the specific case.

As with every referral: records flow through counsel, and no medical records or
other confidential material should be sent before the conflict check clears. A
secure upload link is provided after clearance.

## Medical records by treating specialty

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Emergency, trauma, and initial hospitalization records, including operative reports</li>
  <li>Physiatry and rehabilitation medicine records</li>
  <li>Neurology and neurosurgery records</li>
  <li>Orthopedic records, including hardware and revision history</li>
  <li>Pain management records, including procedure notes</li>
  <li>Urology, gastroenterology, and wound care records, where involved</li>
  <li>Psychiatry, psychology, and neuropsychological evaluation records</li>
  <li>Primary care records for the period before and after the event</li>
  <li>Imaging reports and diagnostic study results</li>
</ul>

Organizing records by specialty matters because future care recommendations
come from specific providers. The plan identifies which provider recommended
each item of care, so the file should make those recommendations findable.

## Rehabilitation and therapy records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Physical therapy evaluations, progress notes, and discharge summaries</li>
  <li>Occupational therapy records, including self-care and home management assessments</li>
  <li>Speech-language pathology and cognitive rehabilitation records</li>
  <li>Inpatient rehabilitation admission and discharge summaries</li>
  <li>Functional capacity evaluation reports</li>
  <li>Driver rehabilitation or adaptive driving assessments, where performed</li>
</ul>

## Medications, supplies, and equipment

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Current medication list with drug, dose, frequency, and prescribing provider</li>
  <li>Pharmacy dispensing printouts for the relevant period</li>
  <li>Durable medical equipment prescriptions and invoices, including wheelchairs and seating systems</li>
  <li>Orthotic and prosthetic records, including fitting and replacement history</li>
  <li>Disposable supply usage records, such as catheter, ostomy, or wound care supplies</li>
  <li>Equipment maintenance, repair, and replacement records</li>
</ul>

Replacement cycles are part of the plan's arithmetic. Invoices and usage
records document what the person actually uses and how often it is replaced,
which grounds frequency and duration assumptions that would otherwise be
estimates.

## Home, attendant care, and environment

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Home health and attendant care records, including hours per day and level of care</li>
  <li>Care agency invoices or caregiver logs, including family-provided care documentation</li>
  <li>Case management notes and care coordination records</li>
  <li>Home accessibility evaluations, modification reports, or contractor bids</li>
  <li>Vehicle modification records or assessments</li>
</ul>

## Insurance, billing, and cost records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Itemized billing records showing charges for treatment to date</li>
  <li>Explanations of benefits from health coverage</li>
  <li>Health plan summaries and coverage documents</li>
  <li>Public benefit records bearing on services received, where applicable</li>
</ul>

These records document historical utilization and cost experience, which inform
the plan's pricing research. How any particular payment source is treated in
the damages presentation is a legal question for counsel; the plan documents
costs and discloses its pricing sources.

## Clinical foundation and future care recommendations

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Treating physician statements or letters addressing future care needs</li>
  <li>Specialist recommendations for future procedures, surveillance, or therapies</li>
  <li>Life expectancy opinions served in the case, if any, and their bases</li>
  <li>Records of scheduled or recommended future surgeries</li>
</ul>

This is the category that carries the plan. A life care plan is a projection
with a clinical spine: each item traces to a documented recommendation or to
collaboration with qualified physicians. Records that capture those
recommendations in the providers' own words are worth specific requests.

## Pediatric case additions

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Growth charts and developmental assessments</li>
  <li>Pediatric specialty records, including neonatal intensive care records in birth injury matters</li>
  <li>School records, including any individualized education program or 504 plan</li>
  <li>Early intervention program records</li>
</ul>

## Prior evaluations and litigation materials

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Life care plans or medical cost projections served by either side</li>
  <li>Vocational evaluations and economic reports served in the case</li>
  <li>Depositions of treating physicians and care providers</li>
  <li>Day-in-the-life or care documentation produced in discovery</li>
</ul>

## How the plan uses this file

The completed plan identifies the records reviewed, states the clinical source
for each recommendation, prices each item from disclosed sources with
geographic adjustment, and presents frequencies and durations that the record
supports. Where the plan feeds a damages calculation, the costing output
becomes a stated input to the forensic economic analysis, which is performed
and signed by independent economist partners coordinated by the firm.
