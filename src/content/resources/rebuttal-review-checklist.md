## How to use this checklist

Read the opposing report once straight through, then work these five layers in
order, marking anything that cannot be traced, checked, or reproduced. The
layers run from the report's inputs to its conclusions: foundation first,
because a methodologically elegant report built on assumed facts fails at the
start; arithmetic late, because math errors are easy to find once the structure
is mapped. Items marked here become the scope of a formal rebuttal and peer
review engagement, which tests the report against its own cited sources and
states which criticisms actually matter to the bottom line.

## Layer one: foundation

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Are the medical restrictions adopted from identified sources in the record, or assumed?</li>
  <li>Is each restriction attributed to a named provider and document?</li>
  <li>Was an interview or evaluation conducted, and is its method described?</li>
  <li>Is the records-reviewed list complete enough to support the opinions offered?</li>
  <li>Is the work history verified against employment and wage records, or taken from self-report alone?</li>
  <li>Are the key assumptions stated expressly, or embedded in the analysis without disclosure?</li>
  <li>For life care plans: does each item trace to a documented clinical recommendation or identified physician collaboration?</li>
</ul>

## Layer two: data vintage and geography

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Is every wage or cost figure tied to a named source with a publication date?</li>
  <li>Was the data current when the report was written, and is a superseded edition being used?</li>
  <li>Does the labor market geography match where the person actually lives and works?</li>
  <li>Are occupational codes disclosed so the classifications can be checked?</li>
  <li>For life care plans: is pricing adjusted to the relevant geographic market, and are the pricing sources identified?</li>
  <li>For economic reports: are the worklife, life expectancy, and statistical tables identified by edition?</li>
</ul>

## Layer three: methodology

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Is the methodology named and described in enough detail to be reproduced?</li>
  <li>Are testing instruments identified, with norms, and were publisher protocols followed?</li>
  <li>Can the transferable skills analysis be traced from work history to identified occupations?</li>
  <li>Is any labor market survey documented: who was contacted, when, and what was asked?</li>
  <li>Are growth, discount, and inflation assumptions supported by cited sources rather than asserted?</li>
  <li>Are the conclusions stated within the discipline's recognized measures, or in invented categories?</li>
</ul>

## Layer four: arithmetic and internal consistency

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Do the tables recompute? Check totals, subtotals, and carried figures across tables.</li>
  <li>Are rates and assumptions used consistently from section to section?</li>
  <li>Is anything counted twice, such as attendant care in both a life care plan and a household services loss?</li>
  <li>Are pre-injury and post-injury scenarios built on symmetric assumptions?</li>
  <li>Do the report's numbers match the author's deposition testimony and prior disclosures?</li>
  <li>Do narrative statements match the tables they describe?</li>
</ul>

## Layer five: role boundaries

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Does the author opine only within the discipline they are qualified in and disclosed for?</li>
  <li>Are medical opinions attributed to physicians rather than originated by the evaluator?</li>
  <li>Is present value calculated by an economist, or by a non-economist author?</li>
  <li>Does the report draw legal conclusions that belong to the court or the trier of fact?</li>
  <li>Does the report acknowledge its limits, or claim more certainty than the method supports?</li>
</ul>

## After the screen

A marked-up checklist tells counsel where the report is soft; it does not yet
say which criticisms survive scrutiny or move the numbers. A formal rebuttal
and peer review engagement takes the marked items, tests them against the
report's own cited data and the case record, quantifies the ones that matter,
and states plainly where the opposing work is sound. That last part is
deliberate: a critique that concedes what is solid is more useful at deposition
and more credible at trial than one that disputes everything.

The firm performs rebuttal and peer review for plaintiff and defense counsel
under the same standard, and reviews of economic reports are performed and
signed by independent economist partners coordinated by the firm. Rebuttal
windows are short; send the complete packet early and flag the deadline in the
referral.
