## How to use this checklist

An economic damages analysis converts documented losses into present value, and
the calculation is only as strong as the records behind it. This checklist
organizes the financial and supporting records the economist will work from.
Print it, mark each item as available, requested, or not applicable, and send
it with the referral.

At Purinton Analytics, economic analyses are performed and signed by
independent economist partners coordinated by the firm. The conflict check,
records collection, and scheduling run through one intake, and the economist's
report identifies its own inputs, including any vocational evaluation or life
care plan prepared in the case. Records flow through counsel, and no
confidential material should be sent before the conflict check clears.

## Earnings and tax records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Federal and state income tax returns, typically five years before the event through the present</li>
  <li>W-2 forms from all employers for the same period</li>
  <li>1099 forms for contract or supplemental income</li>
  <li>Pay stubs showing base pay, overtime, shift differentials, bonuses, and commissions</li>
  <li>Social Security itemized statement of earnings</li>
  <li>Employment contracts or offer letters stating compensation terms</li>
  <li>Collective bargaining agreement wage scales, where applicable</li>
</ul>

The earnings history establishes the pre-event trajectory. Compensation
composition matters: overtime patterns, differentials, and incentive pay can
change the base from which losses are measured, and they only appear when the
underlying pay records are in the file.

## Employment benefits records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Employer retirement plan documents and contribution statements</li>
  <li>Health, dental, and vision coverage summaries and employer premium contributions</li>
  <li>Life and disability insurance provided through employment</li>
  <li>Paid leave policies and accrual records</li>
  <li>Stock, profit-sharing, or deferred compensation plan records, where applicable</li>
</ul>

Employer-provided benefits are a component of compensation. Documenting what
the employer actually contributed supports a loss calculation grounded in this
person's compensation package rather than in generic assumptions alone.

## Self-employment and business records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Business federal tax returns, including schedules, for the relevant years</li>
  <li>Profit and loss statements and balance sheets</li>
  <li>General ledgers or bookkeeping records</li>
  <li>Contracts, invoices, or billing records showing the owner's revenue-generating role</li>
  <li>Records of replacement labor hired after the event</li>
</ul>

Self-employment losses turn on the owner's personal contribution to the
business. Records showing what the owner did, what revenue that work produced,
and what it cost to replace the work after the event let the analysis separate
personal earning capacity from business returns.

## Household composition and services

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Household composition, including ages of all members during the loss period</li>
  <li>Description of household services the person provided before the event</li>
  <li>Documentation of services now hired out, with invoices where available</li>
  <li>Records or testimony describing the person's post-event capacity for household work</li>
</ul>

Household services are valued from what changed in this household, measured
against published time-use data. The case records establish the change; the
statistical sources are disclosed in the report.

## Case-type additions

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Wrongful death: dependency information, survivor details, and funeral or related expense documentation where claimed</li>
  <li>Wrongful death: benefits survivors lost, such as coverage or pension survivorship</li>
  <li>Employment matters: post-separation job search records, interim earnings, and severance documentation</li>
  <li>Employment matters: comparator compensation records produced in discovery, where relevant to the claimed loss period</li>
</ul>

## Expert inputs from other disciplines

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Vocational evaluation establishing pre-event and post-event earning capacity</li>
  <li>Life care plan or medical cost projection, where future care costs are claimed</li>
  <li>Physician opinions on work capacity or life expectancy served in the case</li>
  <li>Opposing economic reports and their appendices, in review or rebuttal engagements</li>
</ul>

The economist's report states its inputs. Earning capacity comes from the
vocational analysis, future care costs come from the life care plan, and each
input is attributed to its source so the trier of fact can see how the pieces
fit together, and so each opinion is defended by the expert who issued it.

## What the economist's report contains

The written analysis identifies the records reviewed, states its assumptions
and their sources, discloses growth and discount choices with support, and
presents losses in present value with the arithmetic shown. Reports are
prepared to be read closely by opposing counsel and opposing experts, whichever
side made the referral.
