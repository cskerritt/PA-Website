## How to use this checklist

A vocational opinion is built on records, and the completeness of the file is
one of the first things opposing counsel will test. This checklist covers the
six categories of records used in a typical vocational evaluation. Print it,
mark each item as available, requested, or not applicable, and send it with the
referral. Not every item applies to every case, and the intake call produces a
records request tailored to the claims actually in dispute.

Two ground rules protect the file. First, records flow through counsel, not
directly from parties or providers to the expert. Second, do not send medical
records, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, or any confidential material
before the conflict check clears. A secure upload link is provided after
clearance.

## Education and training records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>High school transcripts, diploma, or GED records</li>
  <li>Postsecondary transcripts, degrees, and enrollment records</li>
  <li>Vocational or technical training certificates and course records</li>
  <li>Professional licenses, with current status and renewal history</li>
  <li>Industry certifications and the requirements to maintain them</li>
  <li>Apprenticeship or journeyman program records</li>
  <li>Military service records, including the DD-214 and occupational specialty documentation</li>
  <li>Continuing education records tied to the person's occupation</li>
</ul>

Education records establish the baseline: what the person was trained to do,
what credentials remain active, and what retraining paths are realistic. License
status matters as much as the license itself, because a lapsed credential
changes the post-injury occupational base.

## Employment and personnel records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Job descriptions for each position held, as written by the employer</li>
  <li>Personnel file, including performance evaluations and commendations</li>
  <li>Promotion, transfer, and discipline records</li>
  <li>Attendance and leave records for the period surrounding the event</li>
  <li>Union records, including seniority standing and the collective bargaining agreement</li>
  <li>Resumes and employment applications prepared before and after the event</li>
  <li>Employer accommodation records and interactive process documentation, where applicable</li>
  <li>Separation, termination, or resignation documents</li>
  <li>Job search logs, applications, and responses after the event at issue</li>
</ul>

The work history has to be reconstructed as actually performed, not as titles
alone suggest. Job descriptions and personnel files ground the transferable
skills analysis, and post-event job search records are central to any mitigation
question.

## Wage, tax, and benefits documentation

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Federal and state income tax returns, typically the five years before the event through the present</li>
  <li>W-2 and 1099 forms for the same period</li>
  <li>Pay stubs showing base pay, overtime, shift differentials, and deductions</li>
  <li>Bonus, commission, and incentive compensation records</li>
  <li>Employer benefits summaries, including health coverage and retirement contributions</li>
  <li>For self-employment: business tax returns, profit and loss statements, and ledgers</li>
  <li>Social Security itemized statement of earnings</li>
</ul>

Earning capacity opinions are compared against what the person actually earned,
so wage records from more than one source matter. Tax returns, W-2 forms, and
the Social Security earnings record cross-check one another, and discrepancies
among them are addressed in the analysis rather than left for cross-examination
to find.

## Medical and functional records

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Medical records stating diagnoses, treatment course, and prognosis</li>
  <li>Physician work-status notes and written activity restrictions</li>
  <li>Functional capacity evaluation reports</li>
  <li>Independent medical examination reports served by either side</li>
  <li>Physical and occupational therapy notes bearing on function</li>
  <li>Mental and behavioral health records, where the claims place them at issue</li>
  <li>Current medication list, with documented side effects that bear on work activity</li>
  <li>Deposition transcripts of treating providers, if taken</li>
</ul>

The vocational expert does not create medical opinions. Restrictions are adopted
from identified medical sources, and the report states whose restrictions were
used. Clear, attributed restrictions are the single most important input to the
evaluation.

## Disability, benefits, and claim files

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Social Security disability file, including determinations, consultative examinations, and any administrative law judge decision</li>
  <li>Workers' compensation claim file, including impairment ratings</li>
  <li>Short-term and long-term disability claim files and decisions</li>
  <li>Veterans Affairs rating decisions, where applicable</li>
  <li>State vocational rehabilitation agency records</li>
</ul>

Benefit determinations apply their own standards and do not decide the
vocational questions in litigation, but the files behind them often contain
examinations, work history detail, and functional findings that are not
available anywhere else in the record.

## Prior evaluations and litigation materials

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Prior vocational evaluations or vocational expert reports served by either side</li>
  <li>Life care plans or medical cost projections served in the case</li>
  <li>Economic damages reports served in the case</li>
  <li>Deposition transcript of the evaluee</li>
  <li>Discovery responses describing employment history and claimed losses</li>
  <li>The operative complaint or claim documents framing the alleged loss</li>
</ul>

Prior expert materials define what the evaluation must engage with. In rebuttal
engagements, the opposing report and its appendices, data printouts, and CV are
the core of the file.

## If the deadline is short

When a disclosure, deposition, or trial date is imminent, do not wait for a
complete file. Send the medical records that state restrictions, the work
history, and the wage documentation, and identify what else is outstanding. The
firm confirms at intake what can responsibly be completed on the available
record, and rush handling is available when the record supports it.

## How record gaps are handled

No file is ever perfect. The final report lists every record reviewed, states
the assumptions adopted where the record is silent, and identifies the records
that could refine the opinions if produced. That practice protects the opinion
on cross-examination and tells counsel exactly what remains worth pursuing in
discovery.
