## The market a Grand Haven evaluee actually faces

A small city's job market cannot be assumed to mirror the metro average,
and an opinion that pretends otherwise invites impeachment. For Grand
Haven evaluees the firm builds the labor market picture deliberately:
Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for
the Grand Rapids metropolitan area and for Michigan statewide establish
the statistical frame, state workforce agency labor market information
adds the state-level view, and direct labor market research fills in
where the referral turns on the real availability of particular work. The
report then defends its geographic frame explicitly, treating commuting
capacity and local opportunity as findings rather than assumptions. Where
an opposing report has already defined a market, the rebuttal work
examines that definition before anything else, since an unsupported
geographic frame often carries more of an opinion's weight than any
single wage source.

## In-person and remote evaluation

No Grand Haven office exists, and none is implied; the firm's staffed
presence is its Kansas City home office, and meetings anywhere else are
by appointment. That structure is not a limitation so much as a design:
records review, structured interviews, and standardized testing under
publisher-approved remote protocols proceed without travel, which keeps
schedules short and costs predictable. When an evaluation should happen
in person, it happens in the evaluee's own community, with travel
scheduled from Kansas City, and the same arrangements support deposition
and trial attendance when the case calendar requires an appearance.
