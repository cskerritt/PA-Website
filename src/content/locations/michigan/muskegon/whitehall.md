Strip away the caption and a Whitehall injury case turns on a short list
of factual questions: what work the evaluee can still perform, whether
that work exists in the surrounding market, what it pays, and what the
injury will cost in care over a lifetime. Purinton Analytics is retained
to answer exactly those questions, for plaintiff and defense counsel
alike, through
[vocational expert evaluation](/services/vocational-expert-witness/),
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), and
[expert testimony support](/services/expert-testimony-litigation-consulting/).
The answers are documented the same way no matter which side asks:
methods disclosed, sources cited, and reasoning laid out step by step so
the lawyer across the aisle can test all of it.

## Answering the earning-capacity question for Whitehall evaluees

The employability and wage questions in a Whitehall case are answered
against the regional market, not the town line. Vocational findings rest
on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates
for the Muskegon-Norton Shores metropolitan area and for Michigan
statewide, supplemented by state workforce agency labor market
information and direct labor market research where the referral questions
warrant it. The future-care question gets the same locality discipline:
[medical cost projections](/services/medical-cost-projection/) and life
care plans are priced through provider-specific inquiries in the market
where the evaluee will actually purchase care. When counsel needs the
lost-earnings and care-cost findings carried into a full damages model,
the firm offers
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure.
