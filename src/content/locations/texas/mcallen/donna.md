A damages case out of Donna often needs more than a single expert: the
vocational picture, the future care picture, and the economic loss
calculation have to agree with one another, and gaps between retained
experts are where cross-examination lives. Purinton Analytics addresses
that risk structurally. Through
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/),
the vocational and care-planning analyses proceed under a single
engagement while independent economist partners, coordinated by the firm,
perform and sign the forensic economic analysis, so each component rests
on consistent assumptions. Donna counsel on either side of the caption
also retain the components separately:
[vocational expert](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) opinions,
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), and
[forensic economic damages](/services/forensic-economic-damages/)
coordination as the case requires.

## Keeping the analyses consistent

Coordination is a division of labor, not a blending of roles. The
vocational opinion establishes work capacity and earning capacity from
the record and from testing; the life care plan derives future care from
the medical evidence and prices it to the evaluee's own regional market;
and the economist partners take those foundations into a signed economic
analysis of their own. Each expert signs what that expert performed. The
result is a damages presentation whose parts can each survive scrutiny
alone and do not contradict each other when read together.

## Grounding the numbers' foundations locally

Although the economic modeling belongs to the economist partners, its
vocational foundation is built here, and it is built locally. Findings
about what a Donna evaluee can earn rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics
occupational employment and wage estimates for the McAllen metropolitan
area and for Texas statewide, on state workforce agency labor market
information, and on direct labor market research where the referral
questions warrant it. Care pricing likewise comes from provider-specific
inquiries and published fee data for the region. The qualitative rule
holds throughout: every finding is sourced, and nothing is asserted that
the cited record cannot carry.

The firm has no Donna office and publishes no local address; its home
