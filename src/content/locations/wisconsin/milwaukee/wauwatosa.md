Catastrophic-injury litigation turns on a question that outlasts the trial: what will this person's care actually require, year over year, for the rest of a lifetime? For Wauwatosa matters, Purinton Analytics answers that question through [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) grounded in the medical record and the treating providers' recommendations, paired where needed with [medical cost projection](/services/medical-cost-projection/) and vocational analysis. The same plan is written the same way whether plaintiff or defense counsel makes the referral, because a care plan that flexes with the retaining party is not evidence.

## Care needs priced against a real market

A future care opinion is only as strong as the market data beneath it, and the labor component of care, from attendant services to skilled nursing, is a labor market question. The firm frames that analysis with Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Milwaukee metropolitan area and for Wisconsin statewide, alongside state workforce agency labor market information and direct provider and vendor research where the plan requires it. ## How a Wauwatosa evaluation happens

Accuracy about presence comes before anything else: the firm keeps no Wauwatosa office and lists no local address. Work proceeds by appointment, and most of it proceeds remotely, through records review under a defined scope and structured video interviews with the evaluee and, where appropriate, family members and caregivers. When the referral questions call for in-person assessment, the evaluation is conducted in the evaluee's own home or community, and travel is scheduled from the Kansas City home office, as it is for depositions and trial. If economic valuation is needed, the firm arranges [coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/) in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one engagement structure.
