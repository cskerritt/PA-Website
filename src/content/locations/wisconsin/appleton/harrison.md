## Earning capacity across a county line

Harrison illustrates why vocational analysis cannot stop at a municipal boundary. An evaluee may live in Calumet County, hold a job near Appleton, and search for replacement work across the whole commuting region. Vocational findings therefore rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Appleton metropolitan area and for Wisconsin statewide, alongside state workforce agency labor market information. Where the referral questions call for it, direct labor market research verifies which occupations are realistically open to a person with the evaluee's education, work history, and residual capacity. Every source is cited so the analysis can be examined, not merely accepted.

## How Harrison evaluees are seen

The firm keeps no office in Harrison and claims none. Case work is remote-first: records analysis, attorney conferences, and structured interviews proceed by secure videoconference on a schedule counsel controls. In-person evaluation, when the referral requires it, takes place in the evaluee's own community, and testimony appearances are handled by travel arranged from the Kansas City home office. This is the same operating model behind the firm's [national practice](/locations/nationwide/), and it means a Harrison engagement receives the identical process a case in a major metropolitan venue would receive.

## Reports built for cross-examination

A Harrison vocational report defines its scope, inventories the records reviewed, and walks its reasoning from data to conclusion without gaps for opposing counsel to exploit. Life care plans document each projected need against the medical record. When lost earnings must be converted into present-value figures, the firm arranges [coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/) in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one engagement structure, keeping the disciplines aligned without blurring who holds which opinion.
