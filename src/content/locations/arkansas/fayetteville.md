Litigation across the Fayetteville-Springdale-Rogers, AR metropolitan area
draws on Purinton Analytics for
[earning capacity and employability evaluation](/services/vocational-expert-witness/)
in injury and employment litigation,
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) in catastrophic injury
cases, and [rebuttal and peer review](/services/rebuttal-peer-review/) of
opposing vocational and care-planning opinions, on referral from plaintiff
and defense counsel alike. The method does not change with the retaining
party. Each file rests on a documented conflict check, a scope written down
before any opinion work begins, and a records request tailored to the
claims, and the finished report is expected to survive reading by the lawyer
on the other side of the caption. Counsel who retain the firm to respond to
an opposing expert receive the same procedural rigor applied in reverse: the
opposing opinion is tested against its own stated sources and methods.

## How by-appointment service actually works

There is no staffed Purinton Analytics office in Fayetteville, Springdale, or
Rogers, and the firm publishes no street address for this location. What
counsel get instead is a practice built deliberately for distance. Records
review, structured interviews, and standardized testing under publisher
protocols proceed remotely where remote administration is supported, which
covers the early phases of most engagements. When an evaluation is better
performed in person, or when deposition and trial calendars require presence,
travel is scheduled from the Kansas City home office and planned around the
court's dates rather than the firm's convenience.

## Grounding opinions in the regional labor market

Employability and earning capacity conclusions for evaluees in the metro
rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage
estimates for the Fayetteville metropolitan area and for Arkansas statewide,
along with state workforce agency labor market information and direct labor
market research where the referral questions call for it. Care-planning work
is priced to the evaluee's own market using provider-specific inquiries and
published regional fee data, because a plan priced to a national average
describes no market in particular and invites cross-examination the record
cannot answer. Transferable skills analysis follows the same logic, matching
the evaluee's demonstrated work history against occupations that actually
exist in the regional economy rather than against a generic national
listing, and the report identifies its sources so the analysis can be
replicated.
