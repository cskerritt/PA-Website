line. The town's very name ties it to the metro's principal city, and
its workers move through a labor market that pays no attention to the
border between South Carolina and Georgia. That commuting reality is
where a credible earning capacity analysis for a North Augusta evaluee

## Venue in Aiken County and the federal forum

The commute may run to Georgia, but the courthouse stays in South
Carolina. For all its economic orientation toward the principal city,
North Augusta lies in Aiken County, and its state-court matters proceed
in the Circuit Court (Court of Common Pleas). South Carolina's federal
forum is the United States District Court for the District of South
Carolina. Which court actually controls a given engagement, along with
any qualification or registration requirement that court imposes, is
settled during the conflict check and intake rather than assumed, and
the report is drafted so that it reads the same way in either system.
That drafting discipline matters most in the part of the analysis a
cross-border town puts under the greatest pressure: the labor market
evidence.

## Measuring a market that ignores the state line

Post-injury employability for someone living in North Augusta cannot be
assessed from South Carolina data alone, because the jobs realistically
open to that person include positions throughout the metro, in the
market that daily commuters into Augusta already reach. The firm's
vocational findings rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational
employment and wage estimates for the Augusta metropolitan area and for
South Carolina statewide, state workforce agency labor market
information, and direct labor market research where a contested
occupation warrants employer-level verification. What the analysis never
does is quote a market average and stop: the written report ties each
conclusion about this evaluee's restrictions, skills, and placement
prospects to a cited source. The broader regional picture appears on the
[Augusta metro page](/locations/georgia/augusta/).
