Easley belongs to Pickens County, not Greenville County, and that county line
is the right place to start. A case arising in Easley is a Pickens County
case for state court purposes even though the town's economic life is bound
up with the larger metropolitan area, and an expert opinion should keep those
two facts straight: venue follows the county, while the labor market follows
the commute. Purinton Analytics writes its
[vocational](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) and
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) opinions for Easley
matters with exactly that separation in mind, for whichever side of the
caption retains the firm.

## Pickens County venue

State civil claims from Easley are heard in the Circuit Court, sitting as the
Court of Common Pleas, for Pickens County. Federal claims have the United
States District Court for the District of South Carolina as their forum;
whether a particular dispute belongs in state or federal court, and what
expert requirements the controlling venue imposes, is a question resolved
during the conflict check and intake, not one the firm presumes to answer in
advance. Counsel handling related matters elsewhere in the region can consult
the [metro page](/locations/south-carolina/greenville/) for the surrounding
venues and the [South Carolina overview](/locations/south-carolina/) for the
rest of the state.

## A Pickens County worker in a metro market

For employability and earning capacity purposes, an Easley evaluee competes
in the labor market of the whole Greenville, Anderson, and Greer
metropolitan area. The firm's findings draw on the Bureau of Labor
Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for that metropolitan
area and for South Carolina statewide, supplemented by state workforce
agency labor market information and by direct employer inquiry where the
referral questions call for it. The analysis stays qualitative in its public
framing and specific in its citations: every occupational conclusion in the
report traces to a named source that opposing counsel can pull and read.

## Evaluations near home, standards from one office

Easley engagements are handled by appointment. The firm has no staffed
office in the town and publishes no local address, a policy of plain
accuracy that extends to every location page it maintains. In practice most
of the work is remote: records analysis, video interviews, and standardized
testing under remote protocols the test publishers support. When the
questions require an in-person evaluation, it is conducted in the evaluee's
own community, and the expert's travel is arranged from the Kansas City home
office, the single base from which the firm's
[national practice](/locations/nationwide/) operates. One office also means
one report standard, so an Easley report reads like the firm's work anywhere
else, which is precisely the point.

## Care planning priced to the evaluee's market

Catastrophic cases from Pickens County often need future care analysis
alongside the vocational opinion. Life care plans and
[medical cost projections](/services/medical-cost-projection/) are priced to
the evaluee's own market using provider-specific inquiries and published fee
data, because care costs where the evaluee actually lives are the costs the
trier of fact should see.
