What is a Pacolet worker's lost earning capacity actually worth? The question sounds simple and is not. It requires knowing what the person could do before the injury, what residual capacity remains, which occupations accept that capacity, and what those occupations pay in the market the person can reach, each step documented well enough to withstand hostile cross-examination. Purinton Analytics answers that question for Pacolet matters through [vocational expert](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) analysis and [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), on referral from plaintiff or defense counsel.

## The Method Behind the Number the Jury Hears

The analysis proceeds in disclosed stages: a review of medical and employment records against a defined scope, a structured vocational interview, a transferable skills analysis matching residual capacity to occupations, and a wage foundation drawn from Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the relevant area and for South Carolina statewide, with state workforce agency labor market information and direct employer research added where the referral questions require. Nothing in the final opinion arrives without a source, which is what lets it survive the trip from report to deposition to trial intact.

Pacolet is a Spartanburg County town, and its state-court matters are heard in the Circuit Court (Court of Common Pleas) for Spartanburg County. The firm's reports are written for either courtroom and for either side of the caption, which is why the working file is kept auditable end to end.

## A Small Town, a Regional Wage Base

Pacolet itself is a small town, and an honest earning capacity opinion says so while recognizing that its residents draw work from across the [Spartanburg metro area](/locations/south-carolina/spartanburg/). The wage evidence therefore reflects the commuting market rather than the town in isolation, and market scale is described qualitatively, without borrowed statistics or invented local color. That restraint is deliberate: an opinion that overstates a small market's depth hands the other side its best exhibit.
