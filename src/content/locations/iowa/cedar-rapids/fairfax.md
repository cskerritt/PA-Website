Most of the expert work in a Fairfax case never requires anyone to be in
Fairfax. Records arrive electronically, interviews are conducted by
secure video, and the standardized instruments that anchor a vocational
opinion are administered remotely under the publishers' own protocols.
Purinton Analytics has organized its practice around that fact, which is
why a small Linn County community receives the same methodology as a
major city: the same
[vocational expert analysis](/services/vocational-expert-witness/), the
same [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), and the same
[rebuttal and peer review](/services/rebuttal-peer-review/), available
to whichever side of the caption calls first.

## What remote-first means in practice

A Fairfax engagement typically moves through records analysis, a
structured interview, testing, and labor market research without a
single trip, and the file is stronger for it, because time goes into
analysis rather than logistics. When the referral questions do warrant
face-to-face evaluation, the firm conducts it in the evaluee's own
community, and when a deposition or trial requires presence, travel is
scheduled from the Kansas City home office. The firm keeps no staffed
office in Fairfax and publishes no address here; that is stated
directly, in the same spirit that governs the firm's
[national practice](/locations/nationwide/).

## Venue for Fairfax litigation

Fairfax matters filed in state court proceed in the District Court for
Linn County. In the federal system, Iowa's forums are the United States
District Court for the Northern District of Iowa and the United States
District Court for the Southern District of Iowa; which of them controls
a given case, and what registration or qualification the forum expects
of a testifying expert, is determined during the conflict check and
intake rather than assumed. Remote-first preparation changes nothing
about testimony: when the forum requires the expert in the room, the
expert is in the room. The firm's broader Iowa engagements are
described on the [state page](/locations/iowa/).

## The market a Fairfax evaluee actually faces

Fairfax workers draw their livelihoods from across the Cedar Rapids
metropolitan economy, so the firm's employability and earning capacity
findings are built on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment
and wage estimates for the Cedar Rapids metropolitan area and for Iowa
statewide, supported by state workforce agency labor market information
and direct labor market research when the questions require it. Every
finding in the report is tied to its geography and source, allowing
counsel on either side to trace the path from data to conclusion. The
metro-wide framework appears on the
[Cedar Rapids metro page](/locations/iowa/cedar-rapids/).

## From referral to signed report

The process opens with a conflict check on the caption or party names;
[request one here](/refer-a-case/) and include any disclosure,
deposition, or trial dates already set, since the remote-first workflow
is scheduled against the court's calendar from the outset. After
clearance, scope and fee terms are confirmed in writing and a tailored
records request issues. Where economic testimony is also needed, the
firm arranges
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure, keeping a Fairfax file as tightly integrated as
any in the practice.
