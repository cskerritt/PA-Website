Expert deadlines, more than geography, usually decide when counsel reaches out. A Wasco case comes to Purinton Analytics with a disclosure calendar already in motion: a date by which the expert must be named, a date by which the report must be served, a deposition window after that. The practice is organized so that those dates are workable. Intake, conflict clearance, records requests, and drafting are sequenced against the court's schedule from the first call, whether the engagement is a [vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) retention, a [life care plan](/services/life-care-planning/), or a [rebuttal review](/services/rebuttal-peer-review/) of the other side's expert. Plaintiff and defense engagements are handled with the same calendar discipline, in Wasco and across the [national practice](/locations/nationwide/).

## The Disclosure Calendar and the Written Report

A report served on deadline is only valuable if it withstands scrutiny afterward, so speed never substitutes for method. Every Wasco engagement is defined in writing at the outset: the questions to be answered, the records needed to answer them, and the basis on which opinions will rest. Drafts are not circulated for advocacy edits. What counsel receives is a signed opinion with its methodology and sources stated, prepared on the assumption that its most careful readers will be opposing counsel and the court.
