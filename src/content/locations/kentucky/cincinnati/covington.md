A Covington case file often crosses a state line before it reaches a
courtroom. The city sits on the Kentucky side of the Cincinnati
metropolitan area, so a claim captioned in a Kentucky court may still turn
on wages, employers, and medical providers drawn from a labor market that
spans Ohio, Kentucky, and Indiana. Purinton Analytics writes vocational and
life care planning opinions for Covington matters with that cross-state
reality in view: the forum determines the procedural frame, but the
evaluee's actual earning capacity is measured against the market where the
evaluee genuinely competes. Counsel for plaintiffs and counsel for
defendants retain the firm on the same terms, and every engagement begins
with a documented conflict check and a defined scope.

## The Kentucky side of a metro labor market

An earning capacity opinion for a Covington evaluee that relied only on
statewide Kentucky data would miss the point, and one that treated the
evaluee as a resident of the principal city would miss it in the other
direction. Vocational findings here rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics
occupational employment and wage estimates for the Cincinnati metropolitan
area and for Kentucky statewide, supplemented by state workforce agency
labor market information and direct labor market research where the
referral questions warrant it. The written report identifies which sources
describe the metro, which describe the state, and why each applies to this
worker, so that opposing counsel can trace every placement conclusion back
to its data.
