Small labor markets punish careless vocational opinions. In a community the size of Plainview, an expert who simply recites metro-wide job listings has not answered the question a court actually needs answered: what work, at what wage, is genuinely available to this evaluee, with these restrictions, from this place. Purinton Analytics writes its [vocational expert](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) opinions for Plainview matters to that harder question, for plaintiff and defense referrals alike, and applies the same rigor to [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) when future care is at issue.

## Analyzing a small market honestly

Plainview is a small Wabasha County community within the Rochester metropolitan area, so the analysis has to hold both facts at once: a limited local employment base and a larger metro market reachable from it. The firm's findings draw on the Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Rochester metropolitan area and for Minnesota statewide, on state workforce agency labor market information, and on direct employer contact when a specific job-availability question requires primary evidence. Where data thins out at the town level, the report says so plainly and reasons from the metro and state levels, rather than dressing up an assumption as a finding.

## Logistics for a Plainview evaluee

There is no local office and no pretense of a local office; Purinton Analytics states plainly that all Minnesota work is by appointment. Records-based work and interviews are handled remotely whenever that serves the referral questions. In-person evaluation, when needed, happens in the evaluee's own community, which for a rural evaluee is often the difference between an assessment that reflects real daily function and a picture distorted by unfamiliar surroundings. Travel for testimony or in-person work is arranged from the Kansas City home office and scheduled to the court's calendar.
