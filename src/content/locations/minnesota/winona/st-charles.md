## Reading the labor market from St. Charles

Loss-of-earning-capacity opinions for a St. Charles evaluee must reflect where such an evaluee could realistically work, and for a town of this modest size that means the Winona, MN area market, in which Winona, the metro's principal city, supplies much of the employment base. The firm treats market size as a finding to be documented, not an assumption. It grounds its vocational conclusions in Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the applicable area and for Minnesota as a whole, in state workforce agency labor market information, and, where the questions justify it, in direct employer research. The result is a transferable-skills and placeability analysis that opposing counsel can test line by line, which is precisely the point.

## Meeting the evaluee where they are

St. Charles work is handled by appointment; the firm has no staffed office in the town and lists no local address, because it does not claim a presence it does not have. In practice this costs counsel nothing. Records move through secure channels, interviews proceed by secure video where appropriate, and when the referral questions call for an in-person evaluation, the appointment is arranged in or near the evaluee's own community. Depositions and trial appearances are scheduled from the firm's Kansas City home office with the case calendar in mind. When lost earnings must be reduced to present-value figures, counsel can add [coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/) in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one engagement structure. The same model supports referrals [nationwide](/locations/nationwide/).
