## Where an Oxford case is heard

State court matters arising in Oxford are generally venued in the Circuit
Court of Calhoun County, and reports for those dockets are written to the
same standard the firm applies in federal litigation. For federal matters,
Alabama offers the United States District Court for the Northern District of
Alabama, the United States District Court for the Middle District of
Alabama, and the United States District Court for the Southern District of
Alabama as forums; which of them controls a particular Oxford case is
confirmed during the conflict check and intake, along with any
venue-specific registration or qualification requirement, so nothing about
admissibility is left to assumption after retention.

## Oxford's weight in the metro labor market

As a principal community of the Anniston-Oxford area, Oxford supplies a
meaningful share of the jobs an injured worker from Calhoun County could
realistically hold. Vocational findings for Oxford evaluees therefore rest
on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for
the Anniston-Oxford metropolitan area and for Alabama statewide, on state
workforce agency labor market information, and on direct labor market
research when a referral question turns on what specific employers actually
require. Transferable skills are analyzed against the occupations present in
this market, not against a national abstraction, and every source is cited
in the report so opposing counsel can trace each finding to its origin.

## Evaluating an Oxford resident

Most of the file work happens at a distance by design: records review,
structured vocational interviews, and standardized testing under publisher
protocols where remote administration is supported. That approach keeps
expert costs proportionate to the size of the claim. When the referral
questions call for an in-person evaluation, the assessment is conducted in
the evaluee's own community, and when a deposition or trial requires the
expert in an Alabama courtroom, travel is scheduled from the Kansas City
home office with lead time that protects counsel's calendar. The firm keeps
no staffed office in Oxford and publishes no local street address, because
accuracy about presence is part of the same discipline that governs its
opinions.
