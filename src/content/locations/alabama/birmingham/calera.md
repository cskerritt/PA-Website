In a catastrophic injury case involving a Calera evaluee, the vocational question is only part of the damages picture. What the person can still earn matters; what their future care will require matters just as much, and the two analyses have to fit together without one expert leaning on the other's conclusions. ## Care needs and employability, each on its own record

A life care plan for a Calera evaluee is assembled item by item from the medical record and treating-provider input: projected care, equipment, medications, therapies, and replacement schedules, each tied to a documented source rather than a template, so that a reviewing expert on the other side can audit every line. The vocational side of the same file is assessed against the Birmingham metropolitan labor market, using Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the metropolitan area and for Alabama statewide, state workforce agency labor market information, and direct labor market research where the referral questions require it. For a smaller community such as Calera, that regional framing is essential: employability opinions confined to the town itself would understate or distort the market an evaluee genuinely faces. When either analysis must be converted into an economic loss figure, that conversion is not done by the planner or the vocational evaluator; through [coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/), independent economist partners perform and sign the economic analysis while the care-planning and vocational work proceeds under the same engagement structure, keeping each opinion within its author's expertise.
