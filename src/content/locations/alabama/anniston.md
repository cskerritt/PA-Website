## Calhoun County courts and the federal districts

The Anniston-Oxford metropolitan area consists of Calhoun County, and state
court matters are typically venued in the Circuit Court of Calhoun County.
The firm also accepts federal matters throughout the state, including cases
in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Alabama,
the United States District Court for the Middle District of Alabama, and the
United States District Court for the Southern District of Alabama. Because
qualification requirements vary by forum, any venue-specific registration or
qualification requirement is identified and confirmed during the conflict
check and intake process rather than assumed after retention.

## A by-appointment practice, stated plainly

The firm maintains no staffed office in Anniston or Oxford and publishes no
street address for this location. Meetings are by appointment, and most of
the work happens at a distance by design: records review, structured
interviews, and standardized testing under publisher protocols where remote
administration is supported. That model keeps costs proportionate for
smaller-value matters without diluting the methodology applied to larger
ones. When an in-person evaluation is warranted, or when a deposition or
trial date requires the expert in the courtroom, travel is scheduled from
the Kansas City home office with enough lead time to protect counsel's
calendar.

## Researching the Anniston-Oxford labor market

Vocational opinions for evaluees in the metro draw on Bureau of Labor
Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the
Anniston-Oxford area and for Alabama statewide, on state workforce agency
labor market information, and on direct labor market research when the
referral questions require employer-level detail. Life care plans and
medical cost projections are priced against the evaluee's own community
rather than national averages, with each cost source documented in the
report. The goal is an opinion that survives the other side's expert reading
it line by line, which is the standard every report is written to meet.
