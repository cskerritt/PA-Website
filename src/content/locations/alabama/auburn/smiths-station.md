A small town rarely contains its own workforce, and Smiths Station is a
case in point: the jobs its residents hold, and the jobs an injured
resident might still perform, are scattered across the Auburn-Opelika
commuting market rather than concentrated on a main street. That is why a
credible vocational opinion for a Smiths Station case starts with the
commuting market, not the town limits. Purinton Analytics builds its
[vocational expert](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) opinions and
[life care plans](/services/life-care-planning/) on that wider footing for
plaintiff and defense counsel alike.

## Where a Smiths Station case is heard

Smiths Station lies in Lee County, and state-court matters involving its
residents proceed in the Circuit Court serving Lee County. On the federal
side, Alabama offers its district courts as the available forums: the
United States District Court for the Northern District of Alabama, the
United States District Court for the Middle District of Alabama, and the
United States District Court for the Southern District of Alabama. The
firm does not guess at the controlling district for any given matter;
venue, along with any registration or qualification requirement it
carries, is pinned down during the conflict check and intake so the
report is aimed at the right courtroom from the earliest draft. Alabama-wide
venue notes are collected on the [state page](/locations/alabama/).

## Earning capacity in a commuter town

When an evaluee lives in a small community and works across a metro, the
labor market analysis has to follow the commute. The firm's opinions for
Smiths Station evaluees draw on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational
employment and wage estimates for the Auburn-Opelika metropolitan area,
whose principal city is Auburn, and for Alabama statewide, together with
state workforce agency labor market information. Where the referral
questions turn on whether particular work is realistically available,
direct labor market research supplements the published estimates. The
result is a report in which the transferable-skills analysis, the market
data, and the conclusions line up, and in which every source is named so
opposing counsel can check it. The regional picture is described in more
depth on the [Auburn-Opelika metro page](/locations/alabama/auburn/).

## Scheduling around the disclosure calendar

Engagements are calendared backward from the court's deadlines. At
intake, counsel identifies the disclosure, deposition, and trial dates
already set, and the work plan, records requests, evaluation, draft, and
final report, is built to land ahead of them. The firm has no office in
Smiths Station and does not pretend otherwise; conferences and much of
the evaluation work proceed remotely, in-person evaluation happens in
the evaluee's own community, and travel is scheduled from the Kansas
City home office that administers the
[national practice](/locations/nationwide/). When the damages case needs
economic modeling as well, the firm offers
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis alongside the vocational and care-planning work.

## Every engagement begins with a conflict check

Before scope, before scheduling, before any opinion work, comes the
conflict check. Counsel on either side of a Smiths Station matter can
[request a conflict check](/refer-a-case/) with the case caption and
parties, a short description of the claims, and the dates that govern
the case. Clearances come back promptly, and a cleared referral moves
straight into a defined scope and a tailored records request.
