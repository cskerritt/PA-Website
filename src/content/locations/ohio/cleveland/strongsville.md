## What survives cross-examination, and what does not

Opinions fail in predictable ways: undisclosed sources, unstated
assumptions, transferable skills asserted rather than analyzed, care costs
listed without a pricing basis. The firm's method is designed against that
failure list. Each Strongsville report identifies its records, states its
methodology in reproducible terms, and connects every conclusion to
evidence a reader can check. In review engagements the same audit is
applied to the opposing expert's work, and the findings are reported
neutrally: where the opposing opinion is sound, the review says so, because
a critique spent on weak objections costs credibility the case will want
later.

## Venue for Strongsville disputes

Strongsville belongs to Cuyahoga County, and its state-court matters are
heard in the Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas. Ohio's federal trial
courts, the United States District Court for the Northern District of Ohio
and the United States District Court for the Southern District of Ohio,
stand as the alternative forums; which one controls a particular filing is
resolved during the conflict check and intake, along with any
venue-specific qualification requirement. Nothing in the firm's method
changes with the courtroom, which is the point: an opinion that depends on
a friendly forum is not an opinion worth serving.

## Labor market evidence for a Strongsville evaluee

Employability and earning capacity findings rest on Bureau of Labor
Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Cleveland
metropolitan area and for Ohio statewide, joined with state workforce
agency labor market information and, where the questions demand it, direct
labor market research. For a Strongsville resident, the report explains how
the commutable market was bounded and why, since that boundary is where
opposing vocational experts most often part ways. The regional economy is
described in qualitative terms, and no figure appears in an opinion unless
the underlying source carries it.
