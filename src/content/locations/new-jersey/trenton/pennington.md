## From the evaluee outward to the market

The vocational analysis in a Pennington case runs in a deliberate order,
and the order is the method. It begins with the person: work history
reconstructed from the records, transferable skills inventoried, and
medically documented restrictions mapped against the demands of the work
the evaluee performed before injury. Only then does the inquiry turn
outward and ask where those skills can realistically be sold. For a
Pennington resident that question is answered at the scale of the
surrounding labor market rather than at the municipal boundary, and the
data behind the answer is named in the report itself: Bureau of Labor
Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Trenton
metropolitan area and for New Jersey statewide, state workforce agency
labor market information, and direct labor market research where the
referral questions warrant it, each source cited so that the opposing
expert can pull the same tables and check the work.

## How the evaluation reaches the evaluee

Most Pennington engagements begin remotely. Records are reviewed, the
structured vocational interview is conducted by video, and standardized
instruments are administered under publisher protocols validated for
remote use, which spares an evaluee recovering from injury the burden of
travel. When the referral questions require observation that only an
in-person meeting can support, the evaluation is conducted in the
evaluee's own community rather than at some distant office, with travel
scheduled from the Kansas City home office. The firm keeps no Pennington
office and publishes no local street address; presence is by appointment,
and saying so plainly is part of the same candor the reports practice.
Deposition and trial appearances are likewise planned from Kansas City
against the dates counsel supplies.
