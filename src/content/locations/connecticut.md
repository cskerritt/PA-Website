A vocational or life care planning opinion offered in Connecticut will be
read closely by the lawyers on the other side of the caption, and Purinton
Analytics writes every Connecticut report with that reader in mind. The
firm accepts engagements from plaintiff and defense counsel alike, and its
credibility claim is not a marketing position but a working method: a
documented conflict check, a defined scope, a records request tailored to
the claims, and a written opinion that rests on a record both sides can
examine.

## Connecticut courts and coverage

Connecticut state-court matters proceed in the Superior Court, and federal
matters in the United States District Court for the District of
Connecticut. The firm maintains separate pages for the Connecticut areas
where engagements most often arise:
Hartford,
Bridgeport,
New Haven,
Waterbury,
Norwich,
Torrington, and
Putnam. Counsel with a matter outside
those areas should not read the list as a limit; it reflects where
engagements most often arise, and the intake process is identical wherever
in Connecticut a case is venued.

## Remote-first work, by-appointment presence

The firm's home office is in Kansas City, and it keeps no staffed office
anywhere in Connecticut. That fact is stated plainly rather than papered
over, because honesty about presence is part of the same discipline that
governs the reports themselves. What Connecticut counsel get instead is a
remote-first practice: records review, structured vocational interviews,
and standardized testing under publisher protocols where remote
administration is supported, followed by in-person evaluation, deposition,
or trial appearance scheduled from the Kansas City home office when the
referral questions or the court calendar require presence. Vocational
findings for Connecticut evaluees rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics
occupational employment and wage estimates for Connecticut and its metro
areas, state workforce agency labor market information, and direct labor
market research where warranted. When a matter also calls for forensic
economic analysis, the firm offers coordinated damages engagements in
which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure. Connecticut engagements run on the same file
standards as the firm's [nationwide practice](/locations/nationwide/),
so a report prepared for a Superior Court matter is built the same way as
one prepared for any other American venue.

## Qualification and disclosure in Connecticut

Before any opinion is disclosed in a Connecticut forum, the firm confirms
what the venue expects of a retained expert. Any venue-specific
registration or qualification requirement is identified and confirmed
during the conflict check and intake process, and the disclosure package,
including qualifications, prior testimony, and compensation terms, is
assembled to the standard the court and the scheduling order require.
Counsel are never left to discover a qualification issue after the
opinion is served; the question is raised and answered at intake, in
writing, whether the matter sits in the Superior Court or in the federal
district.

## Beginning a Connecticut engagement

Every Connecticut matter starts the same way, whichever party is
retaining: with a conflict check. Submit the caption or party names
through the [conflict check form](/refer-a-case/) and note any imminent
disclosure, deposition, or trial dates, along with the judicial district
or court where the case is pending. The firm responds with a defined
scope, a tailored records request, and a schedule that reflects the
court's deadlines and any travel the engagement will involve.
