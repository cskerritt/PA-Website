Methodology travels; offices do not. That is the premise on which Purinton Analytics serves Sawmills, a small Caldwell County town in the Hickory metropolitan area where no national expert practice keeps a storefront and none should claim to. What the firm brings to a Sawmills case instead is a documented, repeatable process, the same conflict check, the same scope definition, the same sourcing rules applied everywhere, delivered through [vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) work, [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), and [expert testimony and litigation consulting](/services/expert-testimony-litigation-consulting/) for counsel on both sides of the docket.

## How remote-first works for a Sawmills case

The engagement opens with a records request tailored to the claims, so the analysis rests on the actual file rather than summaries. Interviews and counsel conferences proceed over secure video, which for a small-town evaluee usually means less disruption and better attendance than any office visit would. When the questions demand an in-person evaluation, the firm goes to the evaluee, conducting the assessment in his or her own community, with travel run out of the Kansas City home office. Nothing about this model is hidden from the fact-finder: the report says where the work was done and how.
