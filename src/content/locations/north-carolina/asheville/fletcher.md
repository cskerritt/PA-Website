A Fletcher worker's labor market does not stop at the town line. Fletcher
belongs to the metropolitan economy anchored by Asheville, and residents
find work across that commuting region, which is why the vocational
question in a Fletcher case is a regional question. Purinton Analytics
frames it that way from the outset: earning capacity before and after
injury is analyzed against the occupations realistically open to the
evaluee within the area they can actually reach, not against the town
alone and not against a national average that no local worker experiences.
The firm accepts these engagements from counsel on either side of the
caption and writes for the reader who will test every assumption.

## How the regional market enters the opinion

The [vocational analysis](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) rests on
Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for
the relevant area and for North Carolina statewide, supplemented by state
workforce agency labor market information and by direct labor market
research where the referral questions call for it. Scale is described
qualitatively; the opinion never leans on a figure the data does not
support. Transferable skills, education, and residual functional capacity
determine which occupations are in play, and the regional data determines
what those occupations pay and how often they are hiring. When lost
earning capacity is joined to future medical needs, the firm's
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) and
[medical cost projection](/services/medical-cost-projection/) work
proceeds in the same engagement, and
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
bring in independent economist partners who perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure.

## Where a Fletcher case is heard

Fletcher is a Henderson County town, and state-court matters involving
Fletcher parties proceed in the Superior Court of Henderson County. North
Carolina's federal trial courts are the United States District Court for
the Eastern District of North Carolina, the United States District Court
for the Middle District of North Carolina, and the United States District
Court for the Western District of North Carolina; the forum that controls
a specific matter, and any venue-specific qualification requirement, is
identified during the conflict check and intake process. Counsel working
across the region can consult the
[Asheville metro page](/locations/north-carolina/asheville/) and the
[North Carolina state page](/locations/north-carolina/).
