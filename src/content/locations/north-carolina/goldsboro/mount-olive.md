## Wayne County venue and the federal forums

Mount Olive sits in Wayne County, and state-court claims arising in the town
proceed in the Superior Court, where North Carolina's civil rules govern
expert disclosure, deposition practice, and trial testimony. Federal matters
in the state are heard in the United States District Court for the Eastern
District of North Carolina, the United States District Court for the Middle
District of North Carolina, and the United States District Court for the
Western District of North Carolina; which of those forums controls a
particular case, and any registration or qualification requirement the
controlling forum imposes on a testifying expert, is identified and
confirmed during the conflict check and intake process. Venue changes
nothing about the method. Every engagement opens with a documented conflict
check, a defined written scope, and a records request tailored to the
claims, so the opinion that follows rests on a record both sides of the
caption can test.

## A small town inside a regional labor market

The labor market that matters for a Mount Olive evaluee is rarely the town
itself. Residents of a small Wayne County community realistically compete
for work across the surrounding region, including the employment
concentrated around Goldsboro, and an earning-capacity opinion has to define
that market honestly before drawing conclusions from it. Vocational findings
rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage
estimates for the Goldsboro metropolitan area and for North Carolina
statewide, supplemented by state workforce agency labor market information
and direct labor market research where the referral questions call for it.
The analysis names the market it uses, treats the commuting reach realistic
for the particular evaluee as part of the definition, and grounds
transferable-skills conclusions in that defined market rather than in
national averages. Context for the wider area appears on the
[Goldsboro metro page](/locations/north-carolina/goldsboro/).
