The Burlington metropolitan area consists of Alamance County, and engagements

Middle District of North Carolina, the United States District Court for the
Eastern District of North Carolina, and the United States District Court for

[North Carolina page](/locations/north-carolina/) describes how the same

## Appointment-based service, without a storefront

The firm keeps no staffed office in Burlington and publishes no street
address for this location. Meetings are by appointment, and that fact is
stated outright because honesty about presence belongs to the same discipline
that governs the written opinions. What counsel receive in place of a local
storefront is a dependable sequence: conflict check answered promptly, scope
and records request defined at intake, evaluation scheduled in the evaluee's
own community or by secure video under publisher protocols, and travel from
the Kansas City home office arranged whenever deposition, trial, or in-person
evaluation dates require it.

## Grounding opinions in the local labor market

Employability and earning capacity conclusions for Burlington evaluees rest
on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for
the Burlington metropolitan area and for North Carolina statewide, alongside
state workforce agency labor market information and direct labor market
research, including employer contact and job posting analysis, when the
referral questions require evidence of actual hiring. A market of
Burlington's character is not interchangeable with the state's largest
metros, so the analysis is built from data covering this area, and each
source is cited in the report so that opposing counsel can follow the chain
from raw data to conclusion.
