Mitigation runs quietly through much of the litigation that arises in Mocksville. Once liability is joined, the fight often shifts to what the injured or terminated person could reasonably have done next: whether lighter work stayed within their restrictions, whether retraining made sense, whether the job search actually mounted matches the one the law expects. Those are vocational questions with vocational answers, and Purinton Analytics supplies the evidence behind them, whichever side of the caption asks. [Vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) opinions establish or test residual capacity, [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) addresses the care dimension when injuries are severe, and the analysis holds to the record whether it favors the retaining party or complicates their theory.

## Evidence of the Market, Not Impressions of It

A mitigation opinion stands or falls on the market it assumes. For a Mocksville evaluee that market is the regional economy of the Winston-Salem metropolitan area, and the firm measures it through Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for that area and for North Carolina statewide, through state workforce agency labor market information, and through employer inquiry when a specific placement question needs a concrete answer. Where an individual's practical access to the regional market is narrower than the data's coverage, the report documents that limitation and its basis instead of treating the full metro as available. The regional practice appears on the [Winston-Salem metro page](/locations/north-carolina/winston-salem/).
