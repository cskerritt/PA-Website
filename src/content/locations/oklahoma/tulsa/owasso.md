A working life leaves a paper trail: job titles, certifications,
performance records, and the skills each position demanded and taught.
When injury or illness interrupts an Owasso career, that trail is where
Purinton Analytics begins. Transferable skills analysis reads the
evaluee's actual history, identifies the abilities that survive the
documented restrictions, and maps them to occupations that genuinely
exist, so the resulting
[vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) opinion
describes a real person's remaining capacity. Plaintiff and defense
counsel in Owasso matters also retain the firm for
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) and
[medical cost projection](/services/medical-cost-projection/).

## What a work history still supports

The analysis proceeds in a fixed sequence. Records and structured
interviews establish what the evaluee has actually done; standardized
instruments, administered under publisher protocols, measure aptitudes
and interests; and the documented restrictions then filter that
inventory down to work the evaluee can still perform. The discipline
lies in refusing shortcuts at either end: no inflating the skill
inventory to manufacture capacity, and no discarding genuinely
transferable skills to manufacture loss. Where restrictions confine the
evaluee to lighter or sedentary work, the report identifies the
occupations that remain and explains how the work history supports each
match.

An occupation matters only with wages attached, so findings for Owasso

Oklahoma statewide, read with state workforce agency labor market
information and direct labor market research where a referral question
turns on a particular occupation or employer type. The report states
which geography supports each finding. The
[Tulsa metro page](/locations/oklahoma/tulsa/) describes the regional
analysis that Owasso matters draw on.
