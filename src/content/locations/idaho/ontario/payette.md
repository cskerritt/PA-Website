Payette shares its name with the county around it, and litigation that arises here can move through venues on either side of a state line. Purinton Analytics provides [vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) and [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) services to attorneys handling personal injury, workers' compensation, employment, and family law matters involving Payette, Idaho, and it accepts that work from plaintiff and defense counsel alike. The town belongs to the Ontario, OR-ID metropolitan area, whose principal city lies across the border in Oregon, and the practice is structured to follow a case wherever that geography sends it.

## Payette County Venue and Courts

Idaho state-court matters from Payette proceed in the District Court serving Payette County. On the federal side, the United States District Court for the District of Idaho is the forum available for Idaho matters; because the surrounding metro reaches into Oregon, a dispute rooted in Payette can occasionally be captioned across the state line, and the controlling venue for any particular case is identified during the conflict check and intake. The forum does not change the work product. Every report is prepared to the same standard: documented methodology, cited sources, and opinions confined to what the record supports, so that the analysis holds up whether it is read by retaining counsel or picked apart on cross-examination. When counsel need economic loss figures alongside the vocational opinion, the referral can be structured as a [coordinated damages engagement](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/) in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one engagement structure.

## A Labor Market That Crosses the State Line

Describing employability and earning capacity for a Payette resident means describing a commuting market that does not stop at the Idaho border. The relevant question is what work actually exists within reach of the evaluee, and here that reach includes Ontario and the Oregon side of the metro as well as Idaho employers. Vocational findings therefore rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the surrounding area and for Idaho and Oregon statewide, on labor market information published by the workforce agencies of both states, and on direct labor market research with employers where the referral questions warrant it. This is a small, cross-border labor market, and the reports say so plainly rather than importing assumptions built for large metropolitan economies.
