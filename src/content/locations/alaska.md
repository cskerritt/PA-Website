Retaining an expert from outside Alaska raises a practical question before
any methodological one: how will the evaluation, the report, and the
testimony actually get done? Purinton Analytics answers that question at
intake rather than leaving it to be discovered mid-engagement. The firm
accepts vocational expert and life care planning referrals from plaintiff and
defense counsel across Alaska, and every engagement letter defines how each
component of the work will be conducted before the work begins.

## Alaska venues and metro coverage

Engagements are accepted for matters in the Superior Court, Alaska's trial
court, and in the United States District Court for the District of Alaska,
along with workers' compensation and administrative forums. Metro pages
describe the firm's work in Anchorage,
Fairbanks-College,
Juneau, and
Ketchikan. Those pages carry venue and labor
market detail; they do not limit coverage, and matters arising anywhere else
in the state are accepted on the same terms, with the same documented
methodology.

## How a national practice serves Alaska

The practice is remote-first by design. Records review, clinical interviews,
and many standardized vocational instruments can be administered by secure
video under publisher protocols, so most Alaska engagements begin, and many
conclude, without anyone boarding a plane. The written report states how each
component of the evaluation was conducted, so the record is clear about what
was done remotely and what was done in person, and opposing counsel can test
the method rather than guess at it. When the referral questions call for
in-person evaluation, or when deposition and trial dates require presence,
travel is scheduled from the Kansas City home office. The firm keeps no
staffed office in Alaska and publishes no street address for this location;
meetings are by appointment, and plain honesty about presence is part of the
same discipline the reports themselves are built on.

Vocational findings for Alaska evaluees rest on Bureau of Labor Statistics
occupational employment and wage estimates for Alaska and its metropolitan
areas, on state workforce agency labor market information, and on direct
labor market research where warranted, which keeps employability and earning
capacity conclusions tied to the labor market where the evaluee actually
lives and works rather than to a distant average. Where the claims require
economic damages testimony, the firm offers
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure. The broader shape of the national practice, including
remote evaluation protocols and travel logistics, is described on the
[nationwide coverage page](/locations/nationwide/).

## Qualification and disclosure

Counsel evaluating an out-of-state expert should know in advance how
qualification will be handled. Admission of expert testimony is governed by
the forum's rules of evidence, and disclosure obligations differ between
Alaska's state and federal courts, so expert disclosure deadlines, deposition
dates, and trial settings are confirmed at intake and tracked through
delivery. Any venue-specific registration or qualification requirement is
identified and confirmed during the conflict check and intake process, so
neither side encounters a surprise at the admissibility stage.

## Referring an Alaska matter

To begin, [request a conflict check](/refer-a-case/) with the case caption or
party names, and flag any imminent disclosure, deposition, or trial dates.
The firm responds with availability, CVs, and fee information; a records
checklist tailored to the claims follows once the conflict check clears, and
the engagement letter then defines scope, evaluation logistics, and
deliverables in writing before any substantive work starts.
