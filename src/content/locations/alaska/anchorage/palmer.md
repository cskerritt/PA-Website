## Venue context: the Matanuska-Susitna Borough

Palmer lies in the Matanuska-Susitna Borough, and its state-court civil
docket proceeds in the Alaska Superior Court. Where a claim belongs in
federal court, the United States District Court for the District of Alaska
is the forum available in the state; which court controls a given matter,
and any registration or qualification requirement that comes with it, is
identified during the conflict check and intake. Because the same opinion
may be tested in either setting, the firm writes to a single standard: a
methodology section an opposing expert can replicate, sources cited
specifically enough to be pulled, and conclusions that stay inside the
disclosed scope. Related venues across the region are covered on the
[Anchorage metro page](/locations/alaska/anchorage/) and the statewide
[Alaska page](/locations/alaska/).

## Labor market research for Palmer claims

Palmer is a small town inside a much larger metropolitan labor market, and
that mismatch is exactly where careless vocational opinions fail. The
question for a Palmer evaluee is not what jobs exist in the abstract but
what work is realistically available to this person, with these
restrictions, from this community. To answer it, the firm draws on Bureau
of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the
Anchorage, AK metropolitan area and for Alaska statewide, on state
workforce agency labor market information, and on direct employer research
when the referral questions require it. Each report explains the reasoning
that connects the data to the evaluee, distinguishing metro-level findings
from statewide ones so the opinion cannot be caricatured as either
overreaching or parochial.

## How a Palmer evaluation proceeds

There is no Purinton Analytics office in Palmer, and the firm says so
plainly rather than implying a presence it does not have. What counsel gets
instead is a remote-first process that treats distance as a logistics
problem, not a quality compromise. Records review, counsel conferences, and
most vocational interviews are handled by secure video. If the case calls
for in-person assessment, or if deposition and trial dates require the
expert in the room, travel is arranged from the Kansas City home office and
the evaluation itself takes place in the evaluee's own community, where
daily function can be observed in context.
