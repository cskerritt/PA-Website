Most Purinton Analytics engagements from the Waterbury-Shelton
metropolitan area are completed without the evaluator ever renting a
conference room there, and that is by design rather than by compromise.
The practice is built remote-first: records review, structured interviews,
and standardized testing under publisher protocols where remote
administration is supported, with travel reserved for the moments that
genuinely require it. On that foundation the firm offers Waterbury and
Shelton counsel, plaintiff and defense alike,
[vocational expert witness](/services/vocational-expert-witness/) work,
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/), and
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure.

## Venues in the Naugatuck Valley

Engagements are accepted for matters venued in the Superior Court sitting
in the Naugatuck Valley Planning Region and in the United States District
Court for the District of Connecticut. Whichever forum holds the case, the
engagement opens the same way: a documented conflict check, a defined
scope, and a records request tailored to the claims, so the written opinion
rests on a record both sides can examine. The firm's
[Connecticut overview](/locations/connecticut/) covers matters venued in
other parts of the state under the same approach.

## What by appointment means here

The firm has no staffed office in the Waterbury area and publishes no
street address for this location; meetings happen by appointment, in
settings arranged for the specific engagement. Saying so plainly matters,
because a practice that overstates its offices invites the inference that
it overstates its findings. When an evaluation should occur in person, or
when a deposition or trial requires the evaluator in the room, travel is
scheduled from the Kansas City home office and coordinated with counsel
around the court's calendar. The evaluee's community, not the firm's
convenience, determines where in-person work takes place.

## Labor market evidence for the region

Vocational conclusions for evaluees in the Waterbury and Shelton area rest
on Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates
for the metropolitan area and for Connecticut statewide, supported by state
workforce agency labor market information and by direct labor market
research when the referral questions justify it. Life care plans and
medical cost projections are priced to the evaluee's own market using
provider-specific inquiries and published fee data for the region. The
report shows its work: every source is cited, and the path from data to
opinion is written out so that the party across the caption can follow it
and challenge it on the merits.

## Beginning a Waterbury engagement

The opening exchange with the firm is a conflict check, not a strategy
session. [Request a conflict check](/refer-a-case/) with the case caption
and parties, and note any disclosure, deposition, or trial dates already
set, so scheduling commitments can be made honestly. After clearance,
counsel receive a written scope, a records request keyed to the claims,
and a timeline built around the court's deadlines. When a matter reaches
courts outside Connecticut, the firm's
[national coverage](/locations/nationwide/) describes how the identical
remote-first model and report standard extend to venues across the
country.
