The Omaha metropolitan area is a cross-state market: the census definition
takes in counties in both Nebraska and Iowa, and counsel practicing in the
metro routinely litigate in both states' courts. Purinton Analytics
serves plaintiff and defense counsel throughout that market with
[earning capacity and employability evaluation](/services/vocational-expert-witness/)
in injury and employment matters, [life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/)
in catastrophic injury cases, and
[coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure.

## A cross-state venue footprint

On the Nebraska side, engagements are accepted for matters in the District
Courts of Douglas County, Sarpy County, Cass County, Saunders County, and
Washington County, and in the United States District Court for the District
of Nebraska. On the Iowa side of the metro, the firm accepts state-court
matters arising in Pottawattamie County, Harrison County, and Mills County
under the same intake discipline. The methodology does not change with the
caption or the forum: every engagement begins with a documented conflict
check, a defined scope, and a records request tailored to the claims, so
that the written opinion rests on a record either side of the caption can
examine. Counsel whose matters sit elsewhere in the state will find the
broader court landscape on the [Nebraska overview](/locations/nebraska/).

## Meetings and evaluations by appointment

Omaha meetings are by appointment. The firm keeps no staffed office in the
metro and publishes no street address for this location, because accuracy
about offices is part of the same discipline that governs its reports. What
counsel gets instead is a practice built to work at a distance without
losing rigor: records review and report preparation proceed remotely,
vocational interviews are conducted by secure video or in person in the
evaluee's own community, and when deposition or trial dates require
presence, travel is scheduled from the Kansas City home office. Scheduling
is coordinated around counsel's disclosure deadlines rather than around a
local storefront's hours.

## Labor market research for a cross-state metro

Because the Omaha metropolitan area takes in Nebraska and Iowa counties
alike, employability and transferable-skills findings must account for
employment in both the Nebraska and Iowa portions of the market. Vocational findings for Omaha
area evaluees rest on Bureau of
Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for the Omaha
metropolitan area and for Nebraska and Iowa statewide, on state workforce
agency labor market information from both states, and on direct labor
market research where the referral questions warrant it. Each source is
cited in the report so that opposing counsel can trace every finding to the
data behind it, and the same sourcing standard applies whether the opinion
addresses lost earning capacity, mitigation, or the vocational component of
a life care plan.

## Starting an Omaha engagement

The practice is national, and Omaha matters are handled with the same
structure the firm applies across its
[nationwide practice](/locations/nationwide/): conflict check first, then a
defined scope and schedule keyed to the case's deadlines. To begin, submit
the caption or party names through the
[conflict check form](/refer-a-case/) and note any imminent disclosure,
deposition, or trial dates. Once the conflict check clears, the firm
confirms scope, fee structure, and the records needed to open the file,
whether the matter is venued in Douglas County, in one of the metro's Iowa
counties, or in federal court.
