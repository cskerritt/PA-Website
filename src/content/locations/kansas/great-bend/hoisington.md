Hoisington is a small Barton County city, and litigation arising there puts
a hard question to any vocational expert: what does earning capacity
honestly look like for a person whose working life is rooted in a small
central Kansas community? An opinion that assumes the town's economy answers
the question by itself understates the evaluee's options; an opinion that
waves at the wider region without evidence overstates them. Purinton
Analytics is retained by plaintiff and defense counsel alike to answer that
question with documented methodology rather than an assumption in either
direction, through [vocational expert opinions](/services/vocational-expert-witness/)
on employability and earning capacity,
[life care planning](/services/life-care-planning/) in catastrophic injury
matters, and [coordinated damages engagements](/services/coordinated-damages-assessment/)
in which independent economist partners perform and sign the economic
analysis while the vocational and care-planning work proceeds under one
engagement structure.

## Hoisington in the Great Bend labor market

A Hoisington evaluee's realistic job market rarely stops at the town limits.
The employability analysis therefore asks what work is actually open to this
person, with these restrictions and this work history, across the commuting
area that includes Great Bend and the rest of Barton County, and it says so
with evidence rather than impression. The firm grounds those findings in
Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational employment and wage estimates for
the area that includes Great Bend and for Kansas statewide, supplemented by
state workforce agency labor market information and by direct labor market
research when the referral questions call for it. The report explains the
reasoning that connects those sources to this evaluee in this community, so
the opinion withstands cross-examination on the thinness of a rural labor
market instead of inviting it.

## Evaluations for Hoisington evaluees

The firm keeps no office in Hoisington and publishes no local address,
because accuracy about presence is part of the same discipline that governs
its reports. Much of the work proceeds remotely: records review, structured
vocational interviews by video, and written opinions move on the litigation
calendar without travel. When the referral questions call for in-person
evaluation, the firm evaluates the person in their own community rather than
asking a Barton County resident to travel to the expert, and when deposition
or trial dates require presence, travel is scheduled from the Kansas City
home office. Meetings with counsel are by appointment.
